# Live-Stream Commerce and "Live Rips" for Collectibles (2023–2026)

> Method note: These notes come from web search results gathered on 2026-10-03. Full-page fetches were blocked by the network egress proxy for almost every domain (tubefilter, modernretail, espn, si.com, techcrunch, substack, gamblinginsider, help.whatnot.com), so many findings rely on search-result summaries of the cited pages and could not be checked line by line. ## Whatnot: scale, money, categories, fees, formats, live UX, trust & safety

### Takeaway
Whatnot is the clear leader in Western live commerce for collectibles. It did more than $8B GMV in 2025 (up from about $3B in 2024), passed that full-year figure by mid-2026, and was valued at $20B in August 2026. Its model is simple: an 8% commission, fast auctions with countdown timers in a chat-driven live room, free giveaways to build audience, and $10/$10 referral credits. Its biggest weak spot is legal: randomized breaks are being challenged as illegal lotteries.

### Cited Findings
**Scale and funding**
- 2025 GMV was more than $8B, more than double 2024's ~$3B. Black Friday 2025 live sales were $75M, about 3x the year before. — [Ebrun (English)](https://english.ebrun.com/20260211/640428.shtml); [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/whatnot-valuation-soars-investors-bet-livestream-commerce)
- Earlier projections had 2025 GMV at about $6B, and actuals came in higher (~$8B). Some aggregator pages still repeat the $6B projection. — [Hall of Cards (aggregator)](https://hallofcards.app/blog-posts/live-commerce-revolution-whatnot-fanatics-2025) vs. [Ebrun](https://english.ebrun.com/20260211/640428.shtml)
- Series F (October 2025): $225M at an $11.5B valuation, co-led by DST Global and CapitalG, with up to $126M of it in secondary shares. — [Business of Fashion](https://www.businessoffashion.com/news/retail/whatnot-secures-115-billion-valuation/)
- Series G (August 7, 2026): $545M at a $20B valuation, co-led by ICONIQ Growth, Lightspeed and Avra. **Categories**
- Whatnot started in trading cards and expanded into fashion, coins, comics, toys, sneakers, electronics, food and other categories. — [Value Added Resource](https://www.valueaddedresource.net/ebay-live-8x-growth/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whatnot)

**Fees and incentives**
- US commission is 8% of the sale price, plus 2.9% + $0.30 payment processing, for roughly 11–13% all-in. UK/EU commission is 6.67%. — [Vendoo guide](https://blog.vendoo.co/whatnot-app-a-guide-for-sellers-and-buyers); [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/whatnot/)
- Referral program: a new user who signs up through a link gets a $10 credit, and the referrer gets $10 after the new user's first purchase. Referrals are tracked in a Referrals tab. — [Whatnot help: How to buy](https://help.whatnot.com/hc/en-us/articles/360061194792-How-to-buy-on-Whatnot) / [Vendoo](https://blog.vendoo.co/whatnot-app-a-guide-for-sellers-and-buyers)
- Giveaways are free-to-enter listings during a live show. Whatnot picks the winner at random from eligible entrants, and the seller pays shipping. — [Whatnot Seller Academy Go Live Guide](https://selleracademy.whatnot.com/guide); [Whatnot help: list products](https://help.whatnot.com/hc/en-us/articles/9779149424269-How-to-list-products-on-Whatnot)

**Live UX: auctions and timers**
- Auctions run inside a live show with a short countdown timer. In a standard auction, a last-second bid extends the timer. In a "Sudden Death" auction, nothing is added and the auction ends exactly at zero. Sellers can turn Sudden Death on or off. — [Whatnot help: Start an auction](https://help.whatnot.com/hc/en-us/articles/9779931101837-Starting-an-Auction-during-a-Livestream); [Whatnot help: Bid on an item](https://help.whatnot.com/hc/en-us/articles/14932924544141-Bid-on-an-item-during-a-show)
- Sellers can mix auctions, fixed-price "buy now" listings and giveaways in the same show, alongside live chat. — [Whatnot Seller Academy](https://selleracademy.whatnot.com/guide); [Closo seller hub guide (2026)](https://closo.co/blogs/platform-specific-guides/inside-the-whatnot-seller-hub-a-survival-guide-for-live-auctions-in-2)

**Rip and break formats, and policy**
- Whatnot publishes a Card Breaks Policy and a separate Surprise Sets Policy for mystery or random products, plus a Trust Center page on card breaks. The full text could not be fetched. — [Card Breaks Policy](https://help.whatnot.com/hc/en-us/articles/34107485220237-Card-Breaks-Policy); [Surprise Sets Policy](https://help.whatnot.com/hc/en-us/articles/34107510260237-Surprise-Sets-Policy); [Trust Center: Card Breaks](https://trust.whatnot.com/card-breaks)
- Whatnot has a newer "Surprise Products" policy that affects mystery-box sellers, including Pokémon repack sellers. — [PokeMasterMates](https://pokemastermates.com/blogs/news/whatnotsuprisebox-policyupdates)

**Trust and safety allegations** (claims in legal filings, not findings of fact)
- The filings allege shill bidding, and allege that some sellers used inside knowledge of which boxes held the best cards and "leaked" hit locations to favored buyers by DM. — [Sports Illustrated](https://www.si.com/collectibles/whatnot-faces-legal-challenges-over-breaking-practices)
- They also allege Whatnot ran for years without self-exclusion tools, enforceable spending limits or addiction resources. The spending limits added in 2025 are called "inadequate" because users control them and can switch them off. — [Sports Illustrated](https://www.si.com/collectibles/whatnot-faces-legal-challenges-over-breaking-practices)

### Inferences
- Whatnot's growth engine has five parts: a cheap, fast auction loop, a social chat room, free giveaways that pull in viewers, a two-sided referral credit, and many creator-sellers each building a "home" audience. Blind Box can copy the credit and social mechanics without copying the auction mechanic.
- An 8% take rate is the benchmark. If Blind Box ever adds creator storefronts or consignment, creators will compare its economics to 8% + processing.
- The 2025 spending-limit change and the arbitration demands show that responsible-play tools are becoming expected for any random-outcome product. Blind Box should build them in from the start.

### Gaps
- I found no official MAU/DAU or total hours-watched figures. The 650k weekly signups is the only recent official user metric found.
- I found no Whatnot-specific GMV for the Labubu/blind-box or toys category.
- Policy texts (Card Breaks, Surprise Sets) could not be read in full because the fetch was blocked. The specific allowed and banned formats on Whatnot are unverified.

## Fanatics Live, eBay Live and Loupe

### Takeaway
Fanatics Live launched in 2023 and is built around breaks. It bought Europe's Voggt and entered the UK in 2025. eBay Live has grown about 8x year on year to seven markets by mid-2026 but does not disclose absolute GMV. Loupe was an early breaks-only app from 2020–21 that seems to have faded. Incumbents compete through events, collector tours and seller tools, not through fees alone.

### Inferences
- Breaks-only platforms like Loupe lost to general live marketplaces with more demand. A niche app grows faster by bringing in outside audiences, such as TikTok creators, than by trying to become a destination for live streams.
- eBay's "70% more spend from first-time buyers during live events" is a rare published sign that live formats lift conversion and basket size for new buyers.

### Inferences
- **Brand-run lives work:** Pop Mart got LIVE-led growth from a small creator base. A scheduled, branded "drop show" can work without a large affiliate budget. For Blind Box, a weekly live "drop and open" show tied to restocks is the closest analog.
- **TikTok is centralizing randomness:** it now runs giveaways, auctions and random breaks through its own tools. Any Blind Box presence on TikTok Shop (e.g. selling tiers via LIVE) should avoid spin wheels or raffles there. Its "free daily spin" mechanic should stay inside the Blind Box app, not in TikTok lives.
- **Reveal-led products fit lives:** blind boxes sell well in LIVE because the reveal is the content. Blind Box's animated reveal could be built to stream or share cleanly.

## Breaks and group rips: mechanics, legality, lawsuits, platform rules

### Takeaway
In a break, buyers pay for "spots" in a box or case that the breaker opens live. Random-assignment formats are now the main legal target. A California qui tam suit (filed July 2025, later unsealed) against Whatnot and Fanatics Live, plus dozens of consumer arbitration claims, allege unlicensed lotteries and gambling. As of the latest coverage, no court or arbitrator had ruled. TikTok has already restricted the formats.

### Cited Findings
- **Mechanics:** a card break is a livestream sale where buyers buy a share of one or more boxes before the seller opens them on camera. In a random team break, every buyer pays the same price and gets a randomly assigned team, then receives the cards for that team. Breaks let many buyers share an expensive case at a lower cost each. — [Gambling Insider](https://www.gamblinginsider.com/news/196069/tiktok-shuts-sellers-out-of-random-card-breaks-routes-them-through-its-own-tool); [Gambling Insider](https://www.gamblinginsider.com/news/196229/fanatics-live-card-breaks-lottery-lawsuit)
- Other formats named in policies include pick-your-team, player, divisional, draft, bounty, pull-til-you-win, HP battles, king of the hill and train games. — [Fanatics Live App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/fanatics-live-break-your-way/id6450561371); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/tiktok-shop-kills-8-break-formats-and-bans-lucky-spins-under-gambling-rules/)
- **Qui tam suit:** filed in July 2025 under the California False Claims Act and later unsealed. It names Whatnot and Fanatics Live and alleges unlicensed box-break lotteries, unpaid gambling taxes and missing licenses. — [Gambling Insider](https://www.gamblinginsider.com/news/196229/fanatics-live-card-breaks-lottery-lawsuit); [Sports Illustrated](https://www.si.com/collectibles/whatnot-faces-legal-challenges-over-breaking-practices)
- **Arbitration claims:** nearly 70 Whatnot customers filed arbitration demands alleging that randomized card breaks and repack breaks violate California lottery and gambling law, federal RICO and consumer-protection standards (reported March 2026). An earlier report counted about 30. — [Benzinga (Mar 2026)](https://www.benzinga.com/markets/private-markets/26/03/51338094/whatnot-faces-rico-illegal-lottery-allegations-tied-to-sports-card-breaks); [Cardlines](https://cardlines.com/new-legal-action-suggests-whatnot-is-unlawful-gambling-scheme/); [The Hobby Wire (headline: "30 arbitration claims")](https://thehobbywire.substack.com/p/whatnot-faces-30-arbitration-claims)
- **The claimants' theory:** randomized breaks are a game of chance, not a retail sale. Buyers pay for a random chance at a desirable team rather than buying identified goods. — [Cardlines](https://cardlines.com/new-legal-action-suggests-whatnot-is-unlawful-gambling-scheme/)
- **Status:** as of the latest coverage, no arbitrator or court has ruled on whether randomized breaks or repacks are illegal gambling. — [Cardlines](https://cardlines.com/new-legal-action-suggests-whatnot-is-unlawful-gambling-scheme/); [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/future-card-breaking-and-repacks-insights-california-litigation)
- Law-firm commentary flags repacks (re-sealed mystery packs built from loose cards) as facing more legal scrutiny. — [Womble Bond Dickinson](https://womblebonddickinson.com/us/insights/alerts/chase-trading-card-repacks-face-growing-legal-scrutiny); [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/future-card-breaking-and-repacks-insights-california-litigation)
- Mainstream media has covered the question, e.g. ESPN's "Could box breaking be considered gambling or a lottery?" (full article not fetched). — [ESPN](https://www.espn.com/mlb/story/_/id/49098946/could-box-breaking-considered-gambling-lottery)
- **Platform rules so far:** Fanatics Live bans spins, raffles, roulette wheels and third-party randomizers but allows random team breaks. TikTok bans eight-plus formats and channels random team breaks through its own randomizer. Whatnot says gambling is banned and publishes Card Breaks and Surprise Sets policies. — [Gambling Insider](https://www.gamblinginsider.com/news/196229/fanatics-live-card-breaks-lottery-lawsuit); [Gambling Insider (TikTok)](https://www.gamblinginsider.com/news/196069/tiktok-shuts-sellers-out-of-random-card-breaks-routes-them-through-its-own-tool); [Whatnot Card Breaks Policy](https://help.whatnot.com/hc/en-us/articles/34107485220237-Card-Breaks-Policy)
- **Breaker economics:** top breakers are reported to make $15–18M a year with 15–25% net margins. This comes from an unverified aggregator. — [Hall of Cards (aggregator)](https://hallofcards.app/blog-posts/live-commerce-revolution-whatnot-fanatics-2025)

### Inferences
- **Blind Box's legal exposure:** it sells randomized physical items at fixed prices, so it falls under the same "chance plus consideration plus prize" lottery analysis. The common defenses are that every purchase delivers a real item, odds are disclosed, and a free route exists. Blind Box's free daily spin and reward-only coins help only if coins cannot be cashed out and the spin needs no purchase. This needs counsel review, especially for California.
- **Lowest-risk group openings:** formats close to TikTok's allowed list are safest. Each buyer gets their own box ("Personal") or picks a known slot ("Pick Your Team"). The riskiest are random assignment of outcomes between buyers, and add-on chance games such as bounties, wheels and raffles.
- **Product signals:** platforms are moving randomization onto audited, platform-controlled tools. Blind Box should run server-side, logged and auditable randomness, and publish its odds per tier.

### Gaps
- Docket numbers, the court and current status of the qui tam suit could not be confirmed (fetch blocked).
- I found no official statements from state regulators or attorneys general specifically on breaks. Coverage is litigation- and platform-led.
- The 1990s RICO suits against card makers over chase cards are often cited as background, but I did not retrieve a source here.

## Why watching others open is compelling; viewer-to-buyer conversion; engagement

### Takeaway
Watching someone else open a pack works because of anticipation, a curiosity gap and shared social reaction. Live formats convert far better than standard e-commerce, but most of the specific benchmarks come from vendor or marketing compilations rather than audited data. The strongest hard numbers are platform-reported: eBay's 3x sales lift for streaming sellers and 70% higher first-time-buyer spend, Whatnot's 4x first-time buyers on Black Friday 2025, and Pop Mart's 85% LIVE share.

### Cited Findings
- **Anticipation and curiosity:** dopamine rises most in anticipation of a reward (a 2001 *Neuron* study is cited). Unboxing stretches out the reveal to build suspense. Media psychologist Pamela Rutledge ties unboxing to curiosity and "information gap" theory. — [Mental Floss](https://www.mentalfloss.com/article/72336/why-are-we-obsessed-unboxing-videos); [Medium – Cognitive Neuroeconomics on blind boxes](https://medium.com/cognitive-neuroeconomics/rolling-the-dice-on-cute-how-blind-boxes-hack-your-brains-reward-system-63b2446f80ed)
- **Academic research:** there is work on why children watch unboxing videos, and a peer-reviewed study finding that neurophysiologic "immersion" during video watching predicts viewing time and later behavior. — [ResearchGate (unboxing study)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/354193691_Unboxing_the_trend_Understanding_why_children_watch_unboxing_videos_on_youtube); [PMC (immersion study)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9792976/)
- **Conversion benchmarks** (vendor/marketing compilations, lower reliability):
  - Live commerce converts at about 9–30% versus 2–3% for standard e-commerce.
  - Viewer-to-buyer averages about 3–8%, and top streams reach 12–15%.
  - Viewers spend 15–30 minutes per live stream versus about 54 seconds on a typical e-commerce site.
  - About 35% of viewers chat, and viewers who interact convert 2.4x more than passive viewers.
  - The figures are inconsistent across sources.
  - — [Conbersa](https://www.conbersa.ai/learn/live-commerce-roi-benchmarks); [Firework](https://firework.com/blog/live-shopping-vs-traditional-ecommerce); [Sprii](https://sprii.io/articles/live-shopping-vs-traditional); [Marketing LTB](https://marketingltb.com/blog/statistics/live-shopping-statistics/)
- **Platform-reported lifts:**
  - eBay: sellers using Live sell about 3x more, and first-time collectibles buyers spend about 70% more in live events.
  - Whatnot: 4x first-time buyers on Black Friday 2025.
  - — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/06/ebay-continues-to-bet-on-live-shopping-after-record-quarter/); [eMarketer/compilations](https://www.emarketer.com/content/whatnot-valuation-soars-investors-bet-livestream-commerce)
- **Restock scarcity:** Labubu restocks on TikTok LIVE "frequently sell out within minutes". — [Modern Retail](https://www.modernretail.co/operations/pop-marts-labubu-craze-supercharges-tiktok-shop-sales/)

### Inferences
- Several mechanics could transfer to Blind Box:
  - A live "recent pulls" feed with reactions works as a lightweight social proof stream.
  - "Watch party" or shared openings: friends see each other's reveals in real time, and the reveal animation is synced.
  - Scheduled drop events with countdowns, like Whatnot timers and Pop Mart's weekly lives.
  - Creator storefronts or referral codes where a creator's own audience watches them open Blind Box tiers.
- Interaction is the conversion lever (the 2.4x figure, though low reliability). Reactions, chat or "call your pull" predictions during a reveal may matter more than passive view counts.

### Gaps
- I found no audited, platform-published viewer-to-buyer conversion rate for Whatnot, Fanatics or TikTok collectibles lives.
- I found no rigorous study specifically on vicarious pack-opening and purchase intent. The psychology sources are mostly popular-press articles.

## Risks: chargebacks, fraud, regulatory views

### Takeaway
The main documented risks in live collectibles are regulatory and legal: lottery and gambling claims, RICO and consumer-protection arbitration, and gambling-tax claims. Seller fraud is also documented (shill bidding, rigged or "leaked" hits), and problem-spending concerns have pushed platforms toward spending limits. I found little hard data on chargeback rates.

### Cited Findings
- **Legal and regulatory:**
  - The California qui tam suit alleges unlicensed lotteries and unpaid gambling taxes.
  - About 70 arbitration demands cite California lottery law, RICO and consumer protection.
  - Neither has been decided yet.
  - — [Gambling Insider](https://www.gamblinginsider.com/news/196229/fanatics-live-card-breaks-lottery-lawsuit); [Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/markets/private-markets/26/03/51338094/whatnot-faces-rico-illegal-lottery-allegations-tied-to-sports-card-breaks)
- **Fraud allegations:** shill bidding, breakers using inside knowledge of box contents, and hit locations leaked to favored buyers by DM. — [Sports Illustrated](https://www.si.com/collectibles/whatnot-faces-legal-challenges-over-breaking-practices)
- **Problem spending:** allegations that self-exclusion tools and binding spending limits were missing, and that Whatnot's 2025 user-controlled limits fall short. — [Sports Illustrated](https://www.si.com/collectibles/whatnot-faces-legal-challenges-over-breaking-practices)
- **Platform self-regulation:** TikTok's gambling policy bans spins, raffles and wheels, and forces randomization through platform tools. — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/tiktok-shops-gambling-policy-bans-raffles-and-lucky-spins-sellers-beware/); [Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/tiktok-shop-gambling-policy-mystery-box-ban-seller-2026)
- **Repack scrutiny:** repacks (mystery packs assembled by sellers) are drawing more legal attention. — [Womble Bond Dickinson](https://womblebonddickinson.com/us/insights/alerts/chase-trading-card-repacks-face-growing-legal-scrutiny)

### Inferences
- A centralized operator like Blind Box (one seller, its own inventory, server-side randomness) avoids the seller-fraud vector of third-party breakers. Its remaining exposures are odds transparency, the lottery analysis, and chargebacks from "friendly fraud" after disappointing pulls.
- Planned mitigations:
  - publish odds per tier;
  - keep an immutable pull log;
  - allow no cash-out of coins;
  - require age gating (18+);
  - offer user and hard spending limits plus self-exclusion;
  - show clear "you receive a physical figure every time" messaging.
- Adding a social or live layer (shared openings, creator streams) brings back some third-party risk. If creators host openings, the outcomes must still come from Blind Box's own randomizer, not from creator-controlled draws, which is the same lesson as TikTok's Surprise Set rule.

### Gaps
- I found no data on chargeback or dispute rates for live-commerce or blind-box purchases.
- I found no regulator (FTC, state AG, UK Gambling Commission) statement specific to breaks or blind boxes within the time window and the fetch limits of this session.
