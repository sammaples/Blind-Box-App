# Online mystery box and case-opening sites for physical goods (2023–2026): site-by-site teardown

> **Read this first: where the evidence comes from.** The research ran on 2026-10-03. The network proxy blocked direct fetches of the operator sites (packdraw.com, hypedrop.com), Trustpilot, the Apple App Store, GlobeNewswire, courtyard.io and most review domains. So almost every finding below comes from search-engine result summaries and snippets, not from pages I read in full. Most third-party sources in this niche are **affiliate or SEO review sites** that earn referral commission: tech-insider.org, betterchecked.com, unpacked.gg, lines.com, casinorankr.com, track360.io, skincasereviewer.com, packspy.com, coincasinohub.com, deadspin.com/mystery-boxes and others. Their numbers often disagree, and some read as machine-generated. Treat any figure from them as **indicative and unverified**. I flag conflicts where I saw them. The most reliable sources here are trade press, legal press, regulator and court-related items, and the 2019 news coverage.
>
> **Scope note on site names in the brief.** "Loot.com" did not turn up as a prominent mystery-box operator. The closest matches are **loot.gg**, a trading-card pack site, and **lootbox.com**, which sells IRL and CS2 boxes. Both are covered briefly. "Mintpool" did not turn up either; the closest match is **MintPull**, a US card-pack app. "Mystery Box UK-style" is covered through Lootie (UK) and the regulatory notes. I added more prominent players I found: Courtyard.io, Collector Crypt, Cases.gg/Clash.gg, MysteryBrand, DOPA (Japanese "oripa") and IcyBox.

## 1. Business model, box price points, item categories, expected value vs price (house edge)

### Takeaway
All of these sites run the same loop: pay a fixed price, a reel or reveal draws one item from a published list, then you either ship the item or convert it to credit. Prices run from a few cents up to thousands of dollars. Reported or estimated returns to players are roughly 64–90% of the price, depending on whether you count the sell-back floor. The house edge comes mainly from (a) the gap between the listed item value and the sell-back value, and (b) weighting the odds toward low-value items.

### Cited Findings
**PackDraw** (US-facing, crypto-heavy)
- Sells virtual packs for physical prizes: gift cards, electronics, watches, sneakers, plus big-ticket items such as Rolex, designer jewellery and even cars. Trading cards are a major category. — [skincasereviewer](https://skincasereviewer.com/irl/reviews/packdraw); [lines.com](https://www.lines.com/mistery-boxes/packdraw)
- Has operated "since 2023 at real scale." — [packdrawreviews / coincasinohub search summary](https://coincasinohub.com/mystery-review/packdraw/)
- Has a "Draw" mode: a 9-card multiplier mini-game with a $350k maximum win. — [skincasereviewer](https://skincasereviewer.com/irl/reviews/packdraw)
- BetterChecked says PackDraw does not publish per-box drop rates, so you "cannot verify the overall expected value of a given pack without opening many of them." This conflicts with other reviews that describe per-item odds. — [BetterChecked](https://www.betterchecked.com/review/packdraw-test-review)

**HypeDrop** (UK-born, now operated offshore)
- Most-cited price range: about $0.17 to about $1,900, with mainstream sneaker boxes at $19–$129. Another source says $0.12 up to $73,000 "ultra-luxury" boxes. These figures conflict. — [gamechampions](https://www.gamechampions.com/en/reviews/hypedrop/); [unpacked.gg](https://unpacked.gg/mystery-boxes/hypedrop-review/); [boxverdict](https://boxverdict.com/reviews/hypedrop/)
- Categories are sneakers, designer goods, electronics and tech. About 87 boxes, per Tech Insider's August 2026 headline. — [Tech Insider](https://tech-insider.org/mystery-boxes/platforms/hypedrop-review/)
- One review cites an "18% convenience fee on wins." This is unclear and possibly an artefact of the summary, so treat it as unverified. — [search summary of HypeDrop reviews](https://thelowlay.com/reviews/hypedrop/)

**Lootie** (UK, founded 2018)
- Boxes cost $0.99 to over $2,000. Categories: sneakers, electronics, gaming peripherals, streetwear, jewellery and gift cards. — [lines.com](https://www.lines.com/mistery-boxes/lootie); [search summary](https://www.bestmysterybox.online/guides/lootie-review.html)

- Commonly quoted box prices are $50, $100, $250, $500 and $1,000, plus special boxes. — [mymillennialguide](https://www.mymillennialguide.com/icybox-review/); [Tuned & Groomed](https://www.tunedandgroomed.com/article-icybox-review.html)
- Featured brands include Rolex, Patek Philippe, AP, Omega, TAG Heuer and Seiko. Expanding into handbags as of October 2026. — [GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/10/01/3372943/0/en/icybox-expands-from-luxury-watches-into-handbags.html); [Threads partner post](https://www.threads.com/@businessbulls.in/post/DZu_yl6ik3m/what-if-collecting-luxury-watches-felt-like-opening-a-mystery-box-thats-exactly/?hl=en)
- "The expected outcome across enough openings is losing money slowly." — [bynudge](https://bynudge.com/is/icybox-legit)

**Collector Crypt** (Solana "gacha")
- Randomised packs of tokenised Pokémon cards. $50 packs exist. — [CoinGecko](https://www.coingecko.com/learn/what-is-collector-crypt-cards); [Bitquery](https://bitquery.io/investigations/collector-crypt-jupiter-gacha)

**loot.gg**
- Trading-card mystery packs (Pokémon, One Piece, Magic) with transparent drop odds, pack battles and a free welcome pack. — [loot.gg](https://loot.gg/)

**lootbox.com**

**Market-wide estimate**
- Tech Insider's US round-up headline claims "8 Tested, 64% EV," and its explainer cites "30–70% EV." These are affiliate estimates. — [Tech Insider](https://tech-insider.org/mystery-boxes/); [Tech Insider how-it-works](https://tech-insider.org/mystery-boxes/how-mystery-boxes-work/)

### Inferences
- The sites earn their margin from the gap between the listed value and what the player can actually recover: sell-back at roughly 70–90%, shipping fees and withdrawal fees. Blind Box has no sell-back, so its "expected value" can only be stated as retail value against price. Publishing that number honestly (for example, "average retail value of a pull") would set it apart in a niche where users often complain that value is lower than displayed.
- Most competitors sell **mixed-category** boxes: sneakers, tech, cash vouchers. Single-category collectible sites (Courtyard, Collector Crypt, loot.gg, IcyBox) look closest to Blind Box's single-category Bearbrick-style positioning, and they are the ones growing fastest.
- IcyBox uses a "Bronze" entry tier and disclosed minimum and maximum values per tier. That is very close to Blind Box's Bronze-to-Diamond structure, so IcyBox is the most direct structural analogue.

### Gaps
- No operator-published RTP (return to player) or house-edge figures were verifiable directly, because the operator sites were blocked. The Cases.gg 10% figure is second-hand.
- I could not confirm whether PackDraw publishes per-item odds per pack. Sources conflict.

## 2. Odds disclosure and "provably fair" systems

### Takeaway
The standard is a per-item percentage odds table on every box, plus a "provably fair" system. Before the spin the server commits a hash of a secret server seed; afterwards it reveals the seed, and the user combines it with their client seed and a nonce to check the result. Reviewers point out that provably fair only proves a single roll was not altered after the fact. It does not prove the odds table is fair, and none of these sites appears to hold a gaming-lab audit.

### Cited Findings
- **RillaBox**: "The server commits a hashed seed before your spin, reveals the original seed after the outcome." Provably fair "doesn't mean the boxes are good value… The drop rate distribution is set by the operator." — [Tech Insider](https://tech-insider.org/rillabox-review/); [bitcoinist](https://bitcoinist.com/rillabox-review/)
- **Lootie**: SHA-256 provably-fair hashing, with odds disclosed on every item in a box. — [lines.com](https://www.lines.com/mistery-boxes/lootie); [Tech Insider](https://tech-insider.org/lootie-review/)
- **HypeDrop**: runs a provably fair seed checker. One affiliate claims it "logs every drop on-chain." That claim is unverified and possibly a summary artefact. — [search summary of HypeDrop reviews](https://www.gamechampions.com/en/reviews/hypedrop/); [csrated](https://csrated.com/case-opening/hypedrop/)
- **PackDraw**: described as having "a working provably fair system and documented payouts" but "unlicensed." — [BetterChecked](https://www.betterchecked.com/review/packdraw-test-review)
- **Cases.gg**: provably fair draws plus published house-edge documentation. — [skincasereviewer](https://skincasereviewer.com/irl/reviews/casesgg)
- **loot.gg**: "every pack shows exactly what it holds and the odds of each drop"; also provably fair pack battles. — [loot.gg](https://loot.gg/)
- **IcyBox**: users "view the odds" and see a guaranteed watch, with minimum and maximum values disclosed per tier. — [Threads partner post](https://www.threads.com/@businessbulls.in/post/DZu_yl6ik3m/what-if-collecting-luxury-watches-felt-like-opening-a-mystery-box-thats-exactly/?hl=en); [GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/10/01/3372943/0/en/icybox-expands-from-luxury-watches-into-handbags.html)
- **MysteryBrand (2019, historic)**: published odds such as 0.0000018% for a "$250M mansion" in a $12.99 box. The mansion pictured was a Bel Air property the site did not own. This shows that published odds alone do not build trust. — [Newsweek](https://www.newsweek.com/mystery-brand-scam-logan-paul-ricegum-youtube-1278339); [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2019/01/02/jake-paul-ricegum-youtube-mystery-boxes-mysterybrands-keemstar/)
- **Regulatory pressure on disclosure**: Tech Insider reports that a US warning letter to HypeDrop required "headline marketing of jackpot tiers be matched with point-of-decision odds presentation, not just buried in a terms page." This is unverified and I could not find it on any regulator site. — [Tech Insider](https://tech-insider.org/mystery-boxes/platforms/hypedrop-review/)
- **Japan**: a mid-2025 Consumer Affairs Agency guideline reportedly requires explicit probability displays for gacha. This comes from a low-quality source. Separately, the premiums framework caps prizes at the lower of 20 times the transaction value or ¥100,000. That cap is relevant to Japanese "oripa" card-pack apps such as DOPA. — [Catch and Shoot](https://catch-and-shoot.com/the-gacha-reckoning-japans-2025-probability-laws-are-forcing-the-industry-to-show-its-hand/); [monolith.law](https://monolith.law/en/general-corporate/online-game-payment-regulation-unjustifiable-premiums-act)

### Inferences
- Blind Box draws from a **live stock shelf with per-piece rates**. That is a different, and arguably more honest, model than a fixed odds table on a virtual box, because the odds shift as stock depletes. If it adopts provably fair, it needs to commit to a hash of the shelf state at draw time. Otherwise, displaying "live odds" at the moment of purchase is the trust signal to copy.
- Show odds at the point of purchase, not only in the terms. This is both the norm and the direction regulators appear to be pushing.

### Gaps
- I found no evidence of third-party RNG (random number generator) or gaming-lab audits (such as GLI or iTech Labs) at any of these operators.
- I could not view any operator's provably-fair page directly.

## 3. Sell-back, instant buyback and cash-out

### Takeaway
Almost every competitor offers an instant sell-back. Mixed-goods sites typically pay **about 70–78%** of listed value as site credit, while collectible-native platforms pay **85–90%**. Crypto cash-out is common. Lootie is the notable site that sells back **for credit only, with no cash-out**. This mechanic drives much of the stickiness, through re-spinning credit, and also much of the gambling classification risk.

### Cited Findings
- **RillaBox**: sell-back pays roughly 70–78% of listed value depending on category, with "NO FEE." The tested average was 74.2%. — [Tech Insider](https://tech-insider.org/rillabox-review/); [bitcoinist](https://bitcoinist.com/rillabox-review/)
- **HypeDrop**: a 74.4% sell-back is reported, and "contested sell-back valuations" are a recurring Trustpilot complaint theme. — [Tech Insider](https://tech-insider.org/mystery-boxes/is-hypedrop-legit/)
- **PackDraw**: you either ship the item or "exchange the item for site balance and withdraw." Reviewers report fast crypto cash-outs, with a $15 minimum crypto withdrawal. — [coincasinohub](https://coincasinohub.com/mystery-review/packdraw/); [tested.gg](https://tested.gg/site/packdraw)
- **Lootie**: "There is no cash withdrawal and no crypto cash-out." The only options are to ship the item or sell it back for credit. Sell-back below the displayed value is "the single most common complaint." — [Tech Insider](https://tech-insider.org/lootie-review/); [lines.com](https://www.lines.com/mistery-boxes/lootie)
- **Cases.gg**: crypto-only withdrawals, with no fiat. Withdrawals are typically under 1 hour once KYC (know-your-customer identity checks) clears. — [skincasereviewer](https://skincasereviewer.com/irl/reviews/casesgg)
- **loot.gg**: "sell it back for balance, and redeem in crypto." — [loot.gg](https://loot.gg/)
- **IcyBox**: sell-back is about 90% of listed value, and credit can be withdrawn to a bank. Withdrawals cost $2. Users complain about fees on sell-back, shipping and cash-out. — [bynudge](https://bynudge.com/is/icybox-legit); [Tuned & Groomed](https://www.tunedandgroomed.com/article-icybox-review.html); [App Store reviews](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/icybox/id6758816716?see-all=reviews&platform=iphone)
- **Courtyard.io**: every pack comes with a guaranteed instant buyback offer at **90% of fair market value (FMV)**. Users can also list on the marketplace at 0% fee. — [CoinGecko](https://www.coingecko.com/learn/what-are-tokenized-pokemon-cards-tcg); [packspy](https://packspy.com/sites/courtyard)
- **Collector Crypt**: instant buyback at 85% (Elite packs) or 90% (Legendary packs) of market value. Commentators say this "creates a soft price floor… and encourages a feedback loop where users often reinvest their proceeds into buying more packs." — [CoinGecko](https://www.coingecko.com/learn/what-is-collector-crypt-cards); [datawallet](https://www.datawallet.com/crypto/what-is-collector-crypt-cards)
- **UK Gambling Commission view**: where items "are confined for use within the game and cannot be cashed out it is unlikely to be caught as a licensable gambling activity." The Commission is more concerned with secondary markets and cash-out. — [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=7bf1bf2f-3ecc-430b-947f-579ce61629fe); [HSF](https://www.hsfkramer.com/notes/tmt/2019-08/uk-gambling-commission-clarifies-position-on-loot-boxes-in-games-during-dcms-inquiry)

### Inferences
- Blind Box's **no sell-back and no coin purchase** rules remove the main lever regulators use to treat these sites as gambling: converting a prize back into money or money's worth. They also remove the main retention loop competitors rely on. Stickiness will have to come from collecting (set completion, rarity display in the Vault) rather than from re-spinning credit.
- The most common complaint at sell-back sites is "the cash-out value is lower than the displayed prize." Blind Box avoids that complaint category entirely.

### Gaps
- Withdrawal limits (daily or monthly caps) were not found for any operator.
- HypeDrop's sell-back terms (credit only versus cash) were not confirmed from primary sources.

## 4. Shipping model and authenticity

### Takeaway
Most sites hold won items in a site inventory and ship them on request. Fees are often revealed only at checkout or waived above a threshold, and typical delivery is 1–2 weeks. Collectible-native platforms (Courtyard, Collector Crypt) store items in an insured third-party vault indefinitely and treat shipping as optional. Shipping complaints, especially for high-value items, are a top Trustpilot theme.

### Cited Findings
- **HypeDrop**: no shipping fee above an undisclosed value threshold, and a flat fee below it (another source cites $5–$50). Average delivery is 6.4 business days, ranging from 3 to 14 calendar days. Complaint themes include slow shipping on high-value items and KYC friction. — [search summary of HypeDrop reviews](https://deadspin.com/mystery-boxes/hypedrop/); [Tech Insider](https://tech-insider.org/mystery-boxes/is-hypedrop-legit/)
- **RillaBox**: free US shipping on orders over $150. In-stock SKUs typically arrive in 7–14 business days. Complaints mention shipping delays on higher-value items. — [Tech Insider](https://tech-insider.org/rillabox-review/); [track360](https://track360.io/blog/is-rillabox-legit-2026-operator-trust-audit)
- **PackDraw**: "delivery fees only show at checkout." — [coincasinohub / review summaries](https://coincasinohub.com/mystery-review/packdraw/)
- **Cases.gg**: ships to North America and the EU only, tracked and insured, typically in 7–14 days. ID checks apply to high-value items. Customs duties fall on the user, which "won't mark items 'gift'"; users report €40–€120 in fees on luxury sneakers. — [skincasereviewer](https://skincasereviewer.com/irl/reviews/casesgg)
- **Lootie**: reviewers report fast delivery on lower-value items, but also shipping delays and non-delivery. — [Trustpilot via search summary](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/lootie.com)
- **IcyBox**: the user pays shipping if they keep the watch. One documented case came to $31.95 including sales tax; a Bronze-tier review cites shipping of at least $4.44. Shipped watches arrive "with box and papers." — [Tuned & Groomed](https://www.tunedandgroomed.com/article-icybox-review.html); [bynudge](https://bynudge.com/is/icybox-legit)
- **Courtyard.io**: every card is authenticated, insured and stored in a **Brink's-operated vault** for free. Cards can also be shipped worldwide via FedEx. Courtyard partners with the grading company CGC. — [CoinGecko](https://www.coingecko.com/learn/what-are-tokenized-pokemon-cards-tcg); [Courtyard docs](https://docs.courtyard.io/courtyard/resources/courtyard.io-x-cgc)
- **MysteryBrand (2019)**: "many users said they did not receive prizes they won." — [Newsweek](https://www.newsweek.com/mystery-brand-scam-logan-paul-ricegum-youtube-1278339)

### Inferences
- Blind Box's flat-postage "Vault" model echoes the vault idea at Courtyard and Collector Crypt, and avoids the "surprise fee at checkout" complaint seen at PackDraw and IcyBox. Showing the flat postage up front, and saying how long a piece can sit in the Vault, would address the top two shipping complaints.
- Authenticity guarantees in this niche come from third-party grading or vault partners (CGC, Brink's). For Bearbrick-style figures, the equivalent would be sourcing receipts and authorised-retailer provenance.

### Gaps
- I found no stated authenticity guarantee or counterfeit policy for HypeDrop, PackDraw or RillaBox.
- International shipping coverage per site was not verified, except for Cases.gg.

## 5. Rewards: daily free cases, levels, rakeback, affiliates, deposit bonuses, leaderboards, battles, upgraders

### Takeaway
Retention is driven by a casino-style stack. It includes XP levels that unlock better daily free boxes, rakeback, large wager leaderboards and races, case or pack battles (1v1 to team formats), referral codes with deposit bonuses, and multiplier mini-games. PackDraw is the most aggressive example. Lootie and loot.gg lean on battles and user-created boxes.

### Cited Findings
- **PackDraw**:
  - A $100,000 monthly wager leaderboard, a $15,000 weekly race and a $2,500 daily raffle (20 winners, $1,000 top prize). — [skincasereviewer](https://skincasereviewer.com/irl/reviews/packdraw); [packdraw.org leaderboard (affiliate)](https://packdraw.org/leaderboard/)
  - Battle formats: 1v1, 2v2, 3v3, Crazy, Terminal, Jackpot and Borrow, plus the "Draw" 9-card multiplier game. — [skincasereviewer](https://skincasereviewer.com/irl/reviews/packdraw)
  - A daily free case, and daily, weekly and monthly rakeback. Support said the rakeback percentages "are calculated internally… we have no access to that nor the formulas used." — [csgocodehub / coincasinohub summaries](https://www.csgocodehub.com/reviews/packdraw-review)
- **HypeDrop**:
  - XP levels unlock daily free boxes. At Level 100, the guaranteed Daily Free Drop is worth at least $78.
  - Daily bonuses of $0.10–$2 in credit are also cited, which conflicts with the Level 100 figure.
  - Affiliate offer: "3 Free Mystery Boxes + 5% Bonus."
  - — [famuse](https://famuse.co/get-free-boxes-on-hypedrop/); [unpacked.gg](https://unpacked.gg/mystery-boxes/hypedrop-review/)
- **Lootie**:
  - "Mystery Battles": multiple players open boxes at the same time and the highest total value takes all the prizes. There are public and private rooms.
  - A $2 free box via an affiliate code.
  - Custom boxes pay their creators a 0–3% cut.
  - — [lines.com](https://www.lines.com/mistery-boxes/lootie); [unpacked.gg](https://unpacked.gg/mystery-boxes/lootie-review/); [Scoop Empire](https://scoopempire.com/how-to-earn-money-with-looties-custom-boxes/)
- **RillaBox**:
  - A free box on signup. — [unpacked.gg](https://unpacked.gg/mystery-boxes/rillabox-review/)
  - Gave users $5 in balance for 5-star Trustpilot reviews (see section 10). — [track360](https://track360.io/blog/is-rillabox-legit-2026-operator-trust-audit)
- **Cases.gg**: "3 Free Mystery Boxes" via an affiliate code, plus battles. — [unpacked.gg](https://unpacked.gg/mystery-boxes/cases-gg-review/)
- **loot.gg**: a free welcome pack and pack battles. — [loot.gg](https://loot.gg/)
- **MintPull** (US card app): a daily check-in gives free packs, plus limited-time events. — [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.garooms.tcgpackcash.gp&hl=en_US)

### Inferences
- Blind Box's free daily spin and reward-only coins match the "daily free box" norm, which looks like the safest retention mechanic in the stack.
- Wager leaderboards, rakeback, battles and multiplier games are what make PackDraw read as a casino. They are also the features cited in its lawsuits and reviews. Avoiding them supports the mass-market and app-store positioning.
- Mechanics that could be adapted without the gambling look:
  - Lootie-style creator boxes, but curated rather than with a revenue share.
  - Level-gated daily rewards, as at HypeDrop.

### Gaps
- No details were found on upgrader or "upgrade" games at these physical-goods sites. These are common at CS2 skin sites such as Clash.gg, but were not confirmed for physical goods.
- Exact deposit-bonus percentages per site were not verified beyond the affiliate offers above.

## 6. Social and live features: drop feeds, chat, streamers, creator codes

### Takeaway
Live "recent drops" tickers, battles and streamer creator codes are standard. The niche's growth has been tied to influencer promotion, first YouTube in 2019 with MysteryBrand, now Kick and Twitch alongside crypto-casino sponsors. That link is also the main source of legal and regulatory risk: the minors claims and advertising rulings.

### Cited Findings
- **MysteryBrand (Jan 2019)**: paid YouTubers Jake Paul and RiceGum to promote boxes to young audiences. In Paul's video he repeatedly paid $99.99 per box. The backlash forced apologies. — [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2019/01/02/jake-paul-ricegum-youtube-mystery-boxes-mysterybrands-keemstar/); [Tubefilter follow-up](https://www.tubefilter.com/2019/01/07/youtube-mystery-box-jake-paul-ricegum-mystery-brand/)
- **HypeDrop**:
  - In 2023 the UK ASA (Advertising Standards Authority) reportedly ruled against an influencer ad for HypeDrop that "blurred the line with gambling." I could not find the ruling on asa.org.uk, so this is unverified. — [unpacked.gg regulation blog](https://unpacked.gg/blog/where-are-crypto-mystery-box-sites-regulated/)
  - In March 2025 the Washington State Gambling Commission reportedly warned influencers promoting HypeDrop that paid loot boxes are illegal lotteries. Reported only by affiliate sites and not found on wsgc.wa.gov. — [unpacked.gg](https://unpacked.gg/blog/where-are-crypto-mystery-box-sites-regulated/); [Tech Insider](https://tech-insider.org/mystery-boxes/are-mystery-boxes-legal-in-the-usa/)
- **PackDraw**:
  - The owner goes by "DOOM13," is active on X and responds to complaints in public. — [search summary of PackDraw reviews](https://www.packdrawreferral.com/is-packdraw-legit/)
  - Affiliate creator codes such as "BONUSMONEY" feed the leaderboard. — [packdraw.org](https://packdraw.org/leaderboard/)
- **Wider streaming context**:
  - A class action named Adin Ross and Drake over Stake gambling streams on Kick. — [Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/kick/adin-ross-drake-sued-in-class-action-lawsuit-against-stake-over-kick-gambling-streams-3275783/)

## 7. Onboarding, open-animation UX, mobile app vs web

### Takeaway
Two reveal styles dominate:
- **Horizontal reel or spinner**, the CS:GO-style case strip, used by HypeDrop, PackDraw, Cases.gg and Lootie.
- **Pack-rip, card-flip or slab reveal**, used by Courtyard, loot.gg, MintPull and DOPA.

Most of the gambling-adjacent sites are **web-first, typically because app-store policy or payment rules push them there** (this is my inference). The newest consumer-facing entrants are **native apps on the App Store and Google Play**: IcyBox, Courtyard, DOPA and MintPull.

- IcyBox shows that a **guaranteed-item, fixed-tier, odds-shown** app can reach the US App Store top 100 in Shopping in 2026, at least for now. That is a direct precedent for Blind Box's tier structure on mobile.
- Patterns worth copying:
  - Disclosed minimum and maximum value per tier (IcyBox).
  - Odds shown in the box preview before purchase (all sites).
  - Free welcome box on signup, so the first experience is a reveal (RillaBox, loot.gg, Cases.gg).
  - Vault view of owned items (Courtyard).

## 8. Payments, KYC, age gates, geo-blocking

### Takeaway
Sites in the crypto-casino mould (PackDraw, Cases.gg, Clash.gg, loot.gg, Collector Crypt) rely on crypto deposits and withdrawals. HypeDrop, Lootie and IcyBox take cards. KYC is triggered by high-value shipping or withdrawals. Washington State is blocked almost everywhere. PackDraw's terms list the US as restricted even though most of its traffic is American.

### Cited Findings
- **PackDraw**:
  - Terms list the US, plus about 21–22 other jurisdictions, as restricted. Yet it "is marketed to Americans and… most of its visitors are American." — [BetterChecked](https://www.betterchecked.com/review/packdraw-test-review); [search summary](https://www.lines.com/mistery-boxes/packdraw)
  - Users report unauthorised card charges. — [BetterChecked](https://www.betterchecked.com/review/packdraw-test-review)
- **HypeDrop**:
  - Bars users under 18 and residents of 25 countries. — [csrated](https://csrated.com/case-opening/hypedrop/)
  - Its posted US state list (as of 26 May 2026) blocks Washington and Idaho. — [Tech Insider](https://tech-insider.org/mystery-boxes/platforms/hypedrop-review/)
  - Card processing is routed through Omnifarious Services Limited (Cyprus). — [search summary](https://tech-insider.org/mystery-boxes/is-hypedrop-legit/)
  - KYC friction is a common complaint. — [Tech Insider](https://tech-insider.org/mystery-boxes/is-hypedrop-legit/)
- **Cases.gg**: instant crypto deposits and crypto-only withdrawals, with KYC before withdrawals and ID checks on high-value shipments. — [skincasereviewer](https://skincasereviewer.com/irl/reviews/casesgg)
- **Washington State law**:
  - The Commission has read RCW 9.46.0237 broadly since 2016, so staking anything of value on a chance outcome to win something of value counts as gambling. — [Tech Insider](https://tech-insider.org/mystery-boxes/mystery-boxes-by-state/)
  - The Commission states that "no type of gambling activity is legal unless it is explicitly authorized by law," and that online gambling is a class C felony. — [WSGC sweepstakes FAQ](https://wsgc.wa.gov/sweepstakes-faq); [WSGC online gambling FAQ](https://wsgc.wa.gov/online-gambling-faq)
- **California AB 831** (effective 1 Jan 2026):
  - Bans **dual-currency** online sweepstakes casinos, and also targets payment processors, platform providers and media affiliates. — [Deadspin](https://deadspin.com/sweepstakes-casinos/legal-news/gavin-newsom-signs-ab-831-reshaping-californias-sweepstakes-casino-market/); [CA Senate analysis](https://spsf.senate.ca.gov/system/files/2025-07/ab-831-analysis.pdf)
  - MysteryBrand reportedly exited California in February 2026. HypeDrop stayed, with tightened AMOE (alternative method of entry, the free route to enter) disclosures. The law does not name mystery boxes. — [Tech Insider](https://tech-insider.org/mystery-boxes/mystery-boxes-by-state/)
- **Cross-border ID**: the NY minor lawsuit alleges "porous age and geolocation checks that allowed minors to reach Stake via VPNs and mirror sites." This concerns Stake, but it is the theory being applied to the sector. — [PRWeb](https://www.prweb.com/releases/lawsuit-filed-on-behalf-of-minor-allegedly-recruited-into-illegal-offshore-crypto-gambling-302742261.html)

### Inferences
- **Coins**: AB 831 targets *dual-currency* models, where a paid currency comes with free sweeps coins that can be redeemed for prizes. Blind Box's coins cannot be bought, but if they can be redeemed toward physical pieces they may still face scrutiny under sweepstakes or AMOE rules. Legal review per state is needed. **Blocking Washington at launch is the market norm.**
- Card and Apple Pay checkout, rather than crypto, fits mass-market reach. It also brings payment-processor risk policies for "games of chance," which is why HypeDrop routes payments through a separate Cyprus entity.

### Gaps
- Full country and state block lists were not obtained from any operator's terms.
- Apple Pay acceptance per site was not confirmed.
- IcyBox's KYC and age-gate practices were not found.

## 10. Controversies: regulators, lawsuits, Trustpilot and Reddit complaints, gambling classification

### Takeaway
Recurring problems:
- Classification as gambling (Washington; UK advertising rulings).
- Influencer promotion to minors (MysteryBrand 2019; 2026 minors lawsuits naming the crypto-gambling ecosystem, which affiliate sites report includes PackDraw).
- Non-delivery and slow delivery.
- Sell-back value below displayed value.
- Opaque bonus maths.
- Fake or incentivised reviews (RillaBox).

  Blind Box's design (no sell-back, no coin purchase, real stock, published rates) avoids the first and third by construction. The second depends on its marketing choices and age gating.
- Incentivised reviews are a known risk in this niche. A clean, unincentivised review strategy is a trust point Blind Box could advertise.
