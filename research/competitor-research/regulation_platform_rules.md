## 1. US baseline: what makes a mystery box illegal gambling or a lottery, and how courts and regulators treat boxes, breaks and loot boxes

### Takeaway
US gambling and lottery law turns on three elements: **prize, chance and consideration**. A paid random draw always involves chance and consideration, so the fight is over "prize" or "thing of value". Courts have mostly rejected claims against products where the buyer always gets the item they paid for and cannot cash out. Examples are the trading-card cases (Chaset v. Fleer, 9th Cir. 2002) and the in-game loot box cases (Mai v. Supercell, N.D. Cal. 2023). The pressure grew sharply in 2025–2026: New York AG v. Valve (Feb 2026), a Washington class action against Valve (Mar 2026), a California qui tam against Whatnot and Fanatics Live alleging box breaks are unlicensed lotteries, and suits against PackDraw. **Resale and cash-out value** is the main point of attack.

### Cited Findings
- Gambling generally requires all three of prize of value, chance and consideration (payment). — [Walters Law Group, "Mystery Box Gaming Business Model Legal Issues"](https://www.firstamendment.com/mystery-box-gaming-business-model-legal-issues/)
- States analyse breaks and repacks for consideration, chance and a prize of value. If all three exist, regulators or plaintiffs may argue the product is unlawful gambling or an illegal lottery. Repacks that emphasise variable returns and high-value chase cards draw extra scrutiny. — [Womble Bond Dickinson, "The Chase Is On: Trading Card Repacks Face Growing Legal Scrutiny"](https://www.womblebonddickinson.com/us/insights/alerts/chase-trading-card-repacks-face-growing-legal-scrutiny)
- **Chaset v. Fleer/Skybox, 300 F.3d 1083 (9th Cir. 2002):** buyers claimed that randomly inserted "chase" cards made card packs RICO gambling. The case was dismissed for lack of standing because buyers got the benefit of their bargain: a random assortment of cards plus a chance at an insert card. The court never decided whether packs are gambling. — [Chaset v. Fleer/Skybox (FindLaw)](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/us-9th-circuit/1167864.html); [OpenJurist](https://openjurist.org/300/f3d/1083/chaset-v-fleerskybox-international-lp). One search summary wrongly called this a "Fifth Circuit" holding. It is a Ninth Circuit case.
- Academic work argues that the earlier **Schwartz v. Upper Deck** litigation treated chase cards in booster packs as illegal gambling under US law, and draws lessons for loot boxes. — [ResearchGate: "Chase Cards in Booster Packs Constituted Illegal Gambling Under US Law: Implications of Schwartz v Upper Deck Co for the Regulation of Loot Boxes"](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/335227791_Chase_Cards_in_Booster_Packs_Constituted_Illegal_Gambling_Under_US_Law_Implications_of_Schwartz_v_Upper_Deck_Co_for_the_Regulation_of_Loot_Boxes) (only the title was seen; the holding was not verified)
- **Mai v. Supercell Oy (N.D. Cal. 2023):** the court dismissed with prejudice claims that loot boxes were illegal slot machines under California law. Items that can only be used in-game and **cannot be cashed out** are not a "thing of value", and subjective or entertainment value does not count. — [Winston & Strawn](https://www.winston.com/en/the-playbook/loot-box-update-northern-district-of-california-dismisses-with-prejudice-loot-box-suit-against-supercell-oy.html); [CourtListener opinion](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/9602384/mai-v-supercell-oy/)
- **New York AG v. Valve:** filed **25 Feb 2026** in NY Supreme Court, New York County. The AG alleges Counter-Strike, Dota 2 and TF2 loot boxes are illegal gambling and seeks a permanent injunction banning them, plus restitution, damages and disgorgement. The **prize** element rests on players being able to sell skins on the Steam Community Market and third-party markets. That ability is treated as a way to "cash out". Most skins are worth cents and rare ones thousands of dollars. — [NY AG press release](https://ag.ny.gov/press-release/2026/attorney-general-james-sues-game-developer-promoting-illegal-gambling-through); [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/new-york-targets-valves-loot-boxes-illegal-gambling); [Jones Walker](https://www.joneswalker.com/en/insights/blogs/perspectives/new-york-targets-valves-loot-boxes-as-illegal-gambling.html); [Casino.org](https://www.casino.org/news/loot-boxes-back-in-court-as-new-york-ag-sues-valve/)
- In its defence, Valve has argued that mystery boxes are "widely used" in the physical world. — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/valve-argues-mystery-boxes-are-widely-used-in-the-tangible-world)
- **Valve class action:** filed **9 Mar 2026** in W.D. Washington. It alleges a ~$2.50 key-to-open loot box system violates Washington's Recovery of Money Lost at Gambling Act and Consumer Protection Act, plus unjust enrichment. — [KING5](https://www.king5.com/article/news/local/bellevue-based-valve-sued-loot-boxes-claims-built-illegal-gambling-system/281-09993c37-acf6-4f95-b018-40503e4e9782); [Hagens Berman](https://www.hbsslaw.com/press/valve-loot-box-gambling-class-action/consumers-sue-valve-corporation-claiming-illegal-gambling-enterprise-in-video-game-loot-boxes)
- **Whatnot and Fanatics Live qui tam:** filed **July 2025** under the California False Claims Act and later unsealed. It alleges "unlicensed box-break lotteries" and failure to pay gambling taxes and obtain licences. There are 18 named plaintiffs in at least 11 states. California DOJ **declined to intervene**, but relators can proceed. Whatnot denies the claims. — [ESPN](https://www.espn.com/mlb/story/_/id/49098946/could-box-breaking-considered-gambling-lottery); [Gambling Insider](https://www.gamblinginsider.com/news/196229/fanatics-live-card-breaks-lottery-lawsuit)
- Nearly 70 Whatnot customers filed arbitration demands alleging randomized card breaks and repack breaks violate California lottery and gambling law, RICO and consumer-protection standards. As of that writing, **no court, arbitrator, state regulator or AG had ruled** on whether breaks or repacks are gambling. — [Womble Bond Dickinson](https://www.womblebonddickinson.com/us/insights/alerts/chase-trading-card-repacks-face-growing-legal-scrutiny); [Cardplayer](https://www.cardplayer.com/poker-news/1642728-california-lawsuits-allege-sports-card-marketplace-is-illegal-gambling); [National Law Review on California card-break litigation](https://natlawreview.com/article/future-card-breaking-and-repacks-insights-california-litigation)
- TikTok Shop cut sellers off from random card breaks and moved them to its own tool. Breakers have filed antitrust suits against TikTok and Fanatics in C.D. Cal. — [Gambling Insider](https://www.gamblinginsider.com/news/196069/tiktok-shuts-sellers-out-of-random-card-breaks-routes-them-through-its-own-tool); [Gambling Insider (breaker suits)](https://www.gamblinginsider.com/news/196462/tiktok-shop-breakers-banned-fanatics-lawsuits)
- **PackDraw:** a mystery-pack site with an instant sell-back option. It reportedly faces suits in Minnesota (LeMond et al. v. Uphoff et al.) and New York, including one alleging it recruited a minor into offshore crypto gambling, and reportedly operates without a gambling licence. *Low-quality aggregator sources; not verified against court records.* — [BetterChecked](https://www.betterchecked.com/review/packdraw-test-review); [Lines.com](https://www.lines.com/mistery-boxes/packdraw)
- **Courtyard:** sells graded-card mystery packs with an **instant buyback at ~90% of value** and resells bought-back cards in new packs. It raised a $30M round led by Forerunner in July 2025. No lawsuit or regulator action against Courtyard was found. — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/07/24/exclusive-forerunner-leads-30-million-round-in-collectibles-marketplace-courtyard)
- Repacks and mystery boxes are "moving from theoretical concerns to active disputes", and box-breaking operators are advised to make sure every participant receives value. — [Womble Bond Dickinson](https://www.womblebonddickinson.com/us/insights/alerts/chase-trading-card-repacks-face-growing-legal-scrutiny)
- Gaming-enforcement agencies in numerous states have pushed back on mystery-box businesses with threatened charges or subpoenas. — [Walters Law Group](https://www.firstamendment.com/mystery-box-gaming-business-model-legal-issues/)

### Inferences
- Blind Box ships a real figure on every draw, has no buyback and no cash-out. That matches the facts of the defence wins (Chaset's benefit of the bargain; Mai's no cash-out). It does **not** match the facts the 2025–2026 plaintiffs and AGs are attacking (resale and cash-out, chase hype, minors).
- **The weak point unique to physical goods:** Bearbricks have a liquid third-party resale market (eBay, StockX and others). That makes the "prize" element easier to argue than for locked virtual items. NY's Valve theory treats resale on markets the operator doesn't run as a cash-out route. Blind Box cannot fully remove this risk. It can reduce it by not running, endorsing or integrating resale, and by not marketing "flip value".
- The live-shelf design means pull odds change after every draw. That is a disclosure-accuracy issue (see Section 7) and it supports "consumer protection" claims if the published odds go stale.

### Gaps
- None of the opinions or complaints were read in full (fetches blocked). The holdings above come from secondary summaries.
- No ruling was found anywhere in the US on a *physical* online mystery box. The legality of the business is legally untested, not settled.
- Not verified this session: Coffee v. Google (N.D. Cal. 2022) and Taylor v. Apple (9th Cir.), both loot-box suits reportedly dismissed for lack of a "thing of value"; and any state AG action against a physical mystery-box site specifically.

## 2. Does a guaranteed physical item of some value avoid the "prize" element? How do buyback, cash-out, case battles, crypto and paid spins change the analysis?

### Takeaway
The industry's "guaranteed value" defence holds that there is no gambling if every box contains goods with fair market value at least equal to the price. The theory is **relatively untested**. It is strongest when the floor item is worth about the box price and weakest when most pulls are worth far less than the price and a few chase pieces are worth far more. **Buyback or cash-out for money or credit is the single biggest risk factor.** In every major regime (US, UK, Spain's draft law, NY AG's theory, Washington's Kater line), the ability to convert winnings into money is what turns an item into a gambling "prize". Case battles and paid spins add straightforward wagering or lottery elements.

### Cited Findings
- Many sellers rely on a "relatively untested legal theory" that a mystery box is not gambling if the combined fair market value of its contents is at least the amount paid. Example: a $20 box of three random figurines worth at least $20 MSRP. — [Walters Law Group](https://www.firstamendment.com/mystery-box-gaming-business-model-legal-issues/)
- If box price "does not correlate in a fair way" to the advertised prize pool, or odds are not fully transparent, regulators may treat that as misleading marketing. — [Law.com / Holland & Knight coverage](https://www.hklaw.com/en/news/intheheadlines/2025/12/illegal-gambling-class-actions-pile-up-against-firms); [Akiba Law on RillaBox](https://www.akibalaw.com/blog/2025/09/mystery-boxes-brand-names-legal-risk-a-closer-look-at-rillabox/)
- **Cash-out converts items into a prize (US):** in Kater v. Churchill Downs (9th Cir. 2018), Big Fish Casino's virtual chips counted as a "thing of value" under Washington law (RCW 9.46.0285). The chips extended play, and chip transfers between users allowed a de facto cash-out. — [9th Cir. opinion PDF](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2018/03/28/16-35010.pdf); [Justia](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca9/16-35010/16-35010-2018-03-28.html)
- The Big Fish litigation settled for **$155M** ($124M Churchill Downs, $31M Aristocrat). — [GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2020/big-fish-games-pay-155m-tweak-games-part-class-action-settlement-gambling/); [CasinoBeats](https://casinobeats.com/2020/05/26/cdi-and-aristocrat-settle-big-fish-games-litigations/)
- **Cash-out converts items into a prize (UK):** the UK Gambling Commission says "the ability to convert in-game items to cash, or to trade them (for other items of value) means they attain a real-world value and become articles of money or money's worth." Where the provider offers no monetisation and authorises no third-party cash-out, the item is not money's worth. — [House of Commons Library briefing CBP-8498 (Aug 2024)](https://researchbriefings.files.parliament.uk/documents/CBP-8498/CBP-8498.pdf); [UK Gambling Commission, "Loot boxes within video games"](https://www.gamblingcommission.gov.uk/news/article/loot-boxes-within-video-games)
- **Spain's draft law** targets random paid mechanics where rewards "can be exchanged for money or other virtual objects". — [Eurogamer.es](https://www.eurogamer.es/el-gobierno-aprueba-un-anteproyecto-de-ley-para-prohibir-el-acceso-de-menores-a-las-cajas-de-loot); [Congreso BOCG text](https://www.congreso.es/public_oficiales/L15/CONG/BOCG/A/BOCG-15-A-52-1.PDF)
- **Instant buyback models in the market:** PackDraw lets users sell an item back for a buyback offer or ship it ([Lines.com](https://www.lines.com/mistery-boxes/packdraw)). Courtyard offers a ~90% instant buyback ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/07/24/exclusive-forerunner-leads-30-million-round-in-collectibles-marketplace-courtyard)). PackDraw is the one facing suits and minor and crypto allegations ([BetterChecked](https://www.betterchecked.com/review/packdraw-test-review)).
- **Platform benchmark for "guaranteed item" sales:** Whatnot's Surprise Sets policy (effective 10 Mar 2025):
  - Every possible item must be fully disclosed before purchase.
  - No prizes inside sets.
  - No "Chase Spots, Prize Boxes, Randomized Wheels", vending machines, claw machines, wheel spins or raffles.
  - Sellers may not describe the floor, ceiling or average value of a set.
  - Some categories have minimum per-item values (e.g. bags $150, accessories $50).
  - Sources: [Whatnot Surprise Sets Policy](https://help.whatnot.com/hc/en-us/articles/34107510260237-Surprise-Sets-Policy); [Cardlines summary](https://cardlines.com/whatnot-mystery-box-policy-changes/); [Premier Finds summary](https://premier-finds-resale-blog.com/2025/03/10/whatnots-surprise-product-selling-policy-what-sellers-need-to-know/)
- PayPal treats "any other activity with an entry fee and a prize" as gambling-type activity that requires approval, and excludes such activity from Purchase Protection. — [PayPal AUP](https://www.paypal.com/us/legalhub/paypal/acceptableuse-full); [PayPal Help](https://www.paypal.com/us/cshelp/article/what-gambling-activities-does-paypal-prohibit-help391)

### Inferences (feature-by-feature guidance; validate with gaming counsel)

| Feature | Rating | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Paid box that always ships one real figure, per-piece odds published, no buyback (current design) | **Lower risk, not risk-free** | Matches Chaset and Mai fact patterns. The "guaranteed value" theory is untested. Risk rises with the spread between box price and the floor piece's value, and with chase or flip-value marketing. Strongest posture: the floor piece's retail value is about the box price. |
| Coins earned from spend (10/$) and redeemable only for boxes; no purchase, no cash-out | **Lower risk** | Works as a rebate or loyalty programme. Keep coins non-transferable between users. Kater turned on transferability. Show real-money equivalents (EU CPC principles). |
| Free daily spin awarding coins | **Lower to moderate** | No consideration if truly free with no purchase needed. Needs official rules and Apple 5.3 compliance if in-app. It is a "reward for logging in" that the EU KIDS Act proposal would bar for minors. In Japan, prizes tied to purchases are capped premiums. |
| Instant buyback / sell-back for cash or site credit | **High risk, likely gambling** | Supplies the cash-out route that makes the item "money or money's worth" or a "thing of value" in the US (Kater, NY v. Valve), the UK (UKGC) and Spain (draft law). It also likely triggers MCC 7995 and processor prohibitions. |
| Credit or coin cash-out to fiat | **Effectively prohibited without a gambling licence** | Same as above, plus money-transmission and AML exposure. |
| Case battles (users stake paid boxes; winner takes all) | **Prohibited without licence** | Player-versus-player wagering on a chance outcome. Consideration, chance and prize are all present. |
| Crypto deposits | **High risk** | PackDraw's alleged offshore crypto gambling involving a minor is the cautionary example. Adds AML and KYC duties and removes card-network controls. |
| Paid spin wheel / paid re-spins | **Prohibited (illegal lottery) in most US states** | Pay, chance, prize. Whatnot bans randomized wheels outright. |
| "Sweepstakes" free-entry (AMOE) used to legitimise paid draws | **High risk / increasingly banned** | Sweepstakes-casino bans in California, New York and others target dual-currency models, and reach payment processors and vendors (Section 3). |

### Gaps
- No court has decided whether "floor value ≥ price" defeats the prize element for an online physical box.
- No source was found that addresses tiered boxes where the floor piece in a $250 tier is worth less than $250 retail.
- How "retail value" should be measured for designer toys (MSRP versus secondary-market price) was not researched.

## 3. Sweepstakes law and the 2025–2026 sweepstakes-casino bans (relevance to the free spin, coins, and any "free entry" model)

### Takeaway
A wave of state laws from 2025 to 2026 bans **dual-currency "online sweepstakes games"** that simulate casino gaming. Many add **vendor liability** that reaches payment processors, geolocation vendors, media affiliates and promoters. Blind Box's reward-only coins are not that model. But adding purchasable coins plus "free" redeemable coins, or casino-style paid spins, would move toward the target.

### Cited Findings
- **California AB 831:** signed **11 Oct 2025**, effective **1 Jan 2026**. It prohibits operating or supporting online sweepstakes games that use a dual-currency model and simulate gambling. It reaches vendors (geolocation providers, payment processors, content suppliers, media affiliates) that "knowingly and willfully" support them. — [ZwillGen](https://www.zwillgen.com/gaming/californias-ab-831-bans-sweepstakes-casinos-expands-liability-vendors/); [SBC Americas](https://sbcamericas.com/2025/10/14/newsom-signs-california-sweepstakes-ban/)
- **New York S5935:** signed by Gov. Hochul in **December 2025** with immediate effect. It bans operating and promoting online sweepstakes casinos and sportsbooks, with fines **up to $100,000 per violation**. Financial institutions, geolocation providers and media affiliates are also covered. — [Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/articles/new-york-enacts-law-prohibiting-sweepstakes-casinos/); [iGaming Business](https://igamingbusiness.com/gaming/new-york-sweepstakes-casinos-ban-indiana-florida-maine/)
- **Other states:**
  - Montana passed the first ban in May 2025. Connecticut and New Jersey also had bans by 2025.
  - In 2026: Indiana (HB 1052, signed 12 Mar 2026), Louisiana (HB 883 and HB 53, which bring sweeps under anti-racketeering law), Maine (dual-currency, casino-style, fines up to $100k) and Tennessee (signed 22 May 2026).
  - Oklahoma SB 1589 passed by veto override and takes effect 1 Nov 2026.
  - Sweepstakes casinos reportedly cannot legally operate in 14 states as of July 2026.
  - Sources: [CasinoReports 2026 roundup](https://www.casinoreports.com/news/sweepstakes-ban-2026-state-roundup/); [Bright Side of News](https://brightsideofnews.com/gambling/sweepstakes-casino-ban-us-states-2026/) (*affiliate or trade sources; check statute numbers*)
- By mid-2025, regulators in Connecticut, Michigan, Maryland, West Virginia, Louisiana and New Jersey had issued cease-and-desist notices or legislation. Plaintiffs are also suing **payment processors and app stores** as aiders. Counsel advise avoiding states with enforcement. — [National Law Review, "Social casino sweepstakes model under fire"](https://natlawreview.com/article/social-casino-sweepstakes-model-under-fire-what-game-companies-payment-processors); [Law.com / Holland & Knight, Dec 2025](https://www.hklaw.com/en/news/intheheadlines/2025/12/illegal-gambling-class-actions-pile-up-against-firms)
- Apple requires that sweepstakes and contests be sponsored by the developer, with official rules in-app stating Apple is not a sponsor (5.3.1, 5.3.2). — [Apple App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Google requires no-purchase sweepstakes, raffles and contests to state participation rules at first interaction, including entry deadline, award date and number of winners. — [Google Play Real-Money Gambling, Games, and Contests policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9877032?hl=en) (via search summary)

### Inferences
- **Don't sell coins.** Earned-only coins redeemable at 100 coins per $1 sit outside the "dual-currency" definition as summarised. Keep coins earned-only, non-transferable and non-cashable, so they look like a loyalty rebate rather than "sweeps coins".
- The free daily spin is safest if:
  - it awards coins or discounts, not direct high-value prizes;
  - there is no paid re-spin or "spin boost";
  - it has written official rules (eligibility, odds, prize values, limits, void where prohibited).
- If any spin prize has substantial value, check state sweepstakes registration and bonding rules (historically New York and Florida for prize pools above $5,000, and Rhode Island for retail promotions). *Not verified this session.*
- Vendor-liability provisions mean processors and app stores may screen for these models. A design that even looks like a sweeps casino could cost Blind Box its payment rails.

### Gaps
- The exact statutory definitions of "online sweepstakes game" in AB 831, NY S5935 and the 2026 statutes were not read. It is not confirmed whether a non-casino mystery-box draw with earned coins falls outside them.
- Sweepstakes registration and bonding thresholds (NY, FL, RI) could not be verified this session (search budget exhausted).

## 4. State-specific hot spots (Washington, New York, California)

### Takeaway
**Washington** is the highest-risk US state. Its "thing of value" definition is broad, its gambling law lets players recover losses, Kater sits on top, and it now has a 2026 Valve class action. **New York** now has an AG theory treating resale value as a prize and a sweeps ban with $100k fines. **California** has AB 831, the Whatnot and Fanatics qui tam, and arbitrations testing whether breaks are lotteries.

### Cited Findings
- Washington: virtual chips were a "thing of value" because they extended play (RCW 9.46.0285), and the Recovery of Money Lost at Gambling Act allows suits to recover losses. — [Kater, 9th Cir. 2018](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2018/03/28/16-35010.pdf); [WSGC Director's statement on Kater](https://wsgc.wa.gov/news/press-releases/directors-statement-regarding-ninth-circuit-court-appeals-published-decision)
- For years the Washington State Gambling Commission did not enforce gambling law against virtual casinos without cash-out. Kater "broke with that tradition". — [GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2020/washington-state-considers-legislation-protect-gaming-apps-gambling-lawsuits/)
- The Washington AG has sought to end "social casino" apps despite earlier gambling guidance. — [KOMO News](https://komonews.com/news/local/state-ag-seeks-to-end-social-casino-apps-despite-previous-gambling-guidance-washington-state-gambling-commission-playtika-bingo-blitz-big-fish-casino-currency)
- Valve class action (W.D. Wash., Mar 2026) under the Recovery of Money Lost at Gambling Act and the Consumer Protection Act. — [KING5](https://www.king5.com/article/news/local/bellevue-based-valve-sued-loot-boxes-claims-built-illegal-gambling-system/281-09993c37-acf6-4f95-b018-40503e4e9782)
- New York: AG v. Valve resale-as-prize theory (Section 1), and the S5935 sweeps ban (Section 3).
- California: AB 831 (Section 3); the Whatnot and Fanatics Live qui tam and ~70 Whatnot arbitrations under California lottery and gambling law (Section 1).

### Inferences
- Consider launching paid draws without Washington, or getting a Washington-specific opinion first. Geo-blocking by shipping address plus IP is feasible because every box ships physically.
- In New York, avoid anything that facilitates resale (no "list on eBay" buttons, no price-guide integrations, no "market value" badges).

### Gaps
- No Washington State Gambling Commission statement on *physical* mystery boxes was found.
- A claim that 48 states allow mystery boxes and one bans them comes from a low-quality source ([tech-insider.org](https://tech-insider.org/mystery-boxes/are-mystery-boxes-legal-in-the-usa/)). It was not used or verified.

## 5. FTC, consumer protection, COPPA, and selling to teens

### Takeaway
Federal regulators have not called loot boxes gambling. The FTC polices them as **unfair or deceptive practices**. The **Genshin Impact / HoYoverse order (Jan 2025, $20M)** is now the US template: no loot-box sales to **under-16s without parental consent**, accurate odds, disclosed virtual-currency exchange rates, and COPPA compliance. Epic (2022–2023, $245M + $275M) adds the dark-patterns and refunds rules. The amended COPPA Rule has been enforced since 22 Apr 2026.

### Cited Findings
- FTC "Inside the Game" loot-box workshop, **Aug 2019**. The staff perspective paper (**Aug 2020**) flagged three concerns: marketing that encourages overspending, costs masked by confusing currency, and impacts on children and vulnerable people. It noted industry odds disclosures and the ESRB "In-Game Purchases (Includes Random Items)" label. — [FTC press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2020/08/ftc-staff-issue-perspective-paper-video-game-loot-boxes-workshop); [Staff perspective PDF](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/documents/reports/staff-perspective-paper-loot-box-workshop/loot_box_workshop_staff_perspective.pdf); [FTC blog "Loot boxes: What's in play?"](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2020/08/loot-boxes-whats-play)
- **FTC v. Cognosphere (HoYoverse, Genshin Impact)**, settled Jan 2025 (filed 17 Jan, order signed 21 Jan 2025, C.D. Cal.).
  - Allegations: loot boxes were unfairly marketed to children, obscured real costs, and misled players about prize odds. Data from under-13s was collected without parental consent (COPPA).
  - Terms: **$20M**; block **under-16s from purchases without parental consent**; disclose loot-box odds and virtual-currency exchange rates; delete children's data.
  - Sources: [FTC business blog](https://ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2025/01/level-tips-businesses-ftcs-settlement-genshin-impact-developer-hoyoverse); [Pocket Gamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/hoyoverse-agrees-to-20-million-settlement-with-ftc-over-loot-boxes/); [Odin Law](https://odinlaw.com/lessons-to-learn-from-the-ftc-complaint-against-cognosphere-and-genshin-impact-part-i-background-of-coppa-and-loot-boxes/)
- **FTC v. Epic Games** (Dec 2022): **$245M** in refunds for dark patterns plus a **$275M** COPPA penalty.
  - Conduct: confusing button layouts caused unwanted purchases; cancel and refund features were hidden; accounts were locked after chargebacks.
  - Order: obtain express consent before charging; don't lock accounts over disputes.
  - FTC refund payments went out in Dec 2024.
  - Sources: [FTC business blog](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2022/12/245-million-ftc-settlement-alleges-fortnite-owner-epic-games-used-digital-dark-patterns-charge); [FTC Dec 2024 refunds](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/12/ftc-sends-refund-payments-consumers-impacted-epic-games-unlawful-billing-practices); [Loeb & Loeb](https://www.loeb.com/en/insights/publications/2023/01/fortnite-video-game-maker-settles-ftc-privacy-deception-claims-for-record-$520-million)
- **Amended COPPA Rule:** published 22 Apr 2025, effective 23 Jun 2025, compliance deadline **22 Apr 2026**, now enforced. Changes include separate verifiable parental consent for third-party disclosure (e.g. targeted ads), biometric and government-ID data counted as personal information, and data-retention limits. — [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2025/04/ftc-announces-significant-amendments-to-coppa); [Davis Polk](https://www.davispolk.com/insights/client-update/ftc-prioritizes-coppa-enforcement-new-compliance-obligations-take-effect); [Hunton](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/coppa-rule-amendment-compliance-deadline-approaches)
- **State App Store Accountability Acts (age verification through app stores):**
  - **Texas:** in effect after the 5th Circuit stayed the preliminary injunction (28 May 2026, and again for the appeal on 5 Jun 2026).
  - **Utah:** delayed to 6 May 2027 by HB 498, which also removed AG enforcement.
  - **Louisiana:** delayed to 1 Jul 2027 by HB 977.
  - Sources: [Wiley](https://www.wiley.law/alert-Key-Developments-With-State-App-Store-Accountability-Acts-as-Texas-Act-Takes-Effect); [Future of Privacy Forum](https://fpf.org/blog/comparing-enacted-app-store-accountability-acts/); [MoFo](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/251111-texas-targets-app-stores-with-new-accountability-law)

### Inferences
- **Teens:** marketing paid random draws to teens is the most likely way to draw FTC or AG attention. The HoYoverse order sets under-16 parental consent as the floor. Given NY and WA gambling theories plus the EU, Spain and China rules (Sections 9–11), the conservative choice is **18+ for paid draws**. Teens could get non-random direct purchases or browse-only access.
- **Live odds:** the HoYoverse "misled about odds" count makes accuracy as important as disclosure. Because Blind Box's odds shift with live stock, show the odds **at the moment of purchase** and lock them to the transaction.
- **Coins:** show coin prices in dollars as well (HoYoverse exchange-rate term; EU CPC principles).
- **Purchase flow:** use explicit purchase confirmation, no accidental-tap purchases, an easy-to-find refund path, and no account lockouts over chargebacks (Epic).

### Gaps
- FTC Endorsement Guides text (16 CFR Part 255, revised 2023) and the 2024 Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule could not be fetched. See Section 13.
- No FTC action against a *physical* mystery-box seller was found.

## 6. Apple App Store

### Takeaway
Physical goods **must not** use Apple IAP; use Apple Pay or card entry instead (3.1.3(e)). Randomized purchases must disclose odds before purchase (3.1.1, written for "virtual items" but a sensible baseline). Anything App Review sees as **real-money gaming or a lottery** needs licences, geo-restriction and a free app (5.3.4). Loot boxes now carry at least a **9+** global rating, **16+ in Australia** and **A18 in Brazil**.

### Cited Findings (verbatim from Apple's guidelines page)
- 3.1.1: "Apps offering 'loot boxes' or other mechanisms that provide randomized virtual items for purchase must disclose the odds of receiving each type of item to customers prior to purchase." — [Apple App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- 3.1.3(e): "If your app enables people to purchase physical goods or services that will be consumed outside of the app, you must use purchase methods other than in-app purchase to collect those payments, such as Apple Pay or traditional credit card entry." — [same](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- 5.3.1: "Sweepstakes and contests must be sponsored by the developer of the app." 5.3.2: "Official rules for sweepstakes, contests, and raffles must be presented in the app and make clear that Apple is not a sponsor or involved in the activity in any manner." 5.3.3: "Apps may not use in-app purchase to purchase credit or currency for use in conjunction with real money gaming of any kind." 5.3.4: "Apps that offer real money gaming (e.g. sports betting, poker, casino games, horse racing) or lotteries must have necessary licensing and permissions in the locations where the app is used, must be geo-restricted to those locations, and must be free on the App Store… Lottery apps must have consideration, chance, and a prize." — [same](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- **Age ratings for chance-based activities:**

  | Region | Rating | Applies to |
  |---|---|---|
  | Global | 4+ | Infrequent contests |
  | Global | **9+** | **Loot boxes (any frequency)** |
  | Global | 13+ | Infrequent simulated gambling; frequent contests |
  | Global | **18+** | **Gambling (real-money betting)**; frequent simulated gambling |
  | Australia | **16+** | Loot boxes |
  | Australia | R18+ | Infrequent simulated gambling |
  | Brazil | **A18** | Loot boxes; frequent gambling |
  | Korea | All | Loot boxes |
  | Korea | — | Frequent or intense simulated gambling needs a Rating Classification Number |

  — [Apple, Age ratings values and definitions](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/app-information/age-ratings-values-and-definitions)
- Apple's 18 Jun 2024 notice gave **15+** for purchasable loot boxes in Australia and asked developers to declare purchasable loot boxes in App Store Connect. The current definitions page says **16+**, which probably reflects the 2025 age-band overhaul. **The two Apple sources differ; use the current page.** — [Apple Developer News, 18 Jun 2024](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=7byvco78)
- Apple's 2025 overhaul added 13+, 16+ and 18+ bands and a longer questionnaire. Completing it was mandatory by 31 Jan 2026 for new submissions and updates. — [Apple Developer News](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=ks775ehf); [ptkd.com](https://ptkd.com/journal/app-store-age-ratings-2025-update) (secondary)

### Inferences
- Blind Box should **not** use IAP for boxes. They are physical goods, and 5.3.3 also bars IAP for real-money-gaming currency.
- The main App Review risk is being classed as a **lottery under 5.3.4** (pay, chance, prize) rather than a loot box. Mitigations:
  - frame boxes as product purchases with a guaranteed item;
  - publish odds;
  - no cash-out;
  - put the free spin's official rules in-app and state Apple is not a sponsor.
- Declare "loot boxes" in the age questionnaire. Expect 9+ or more globally, 16+ in Australia and A18 in Brazil. A self-imposed 18+ is consistent with that.
- Buyback, cash-out, case battles or paid spins would very likely make the app real-money gaming, which needs licences in every jurisdiction served.

### Gaps
- No public App Review decision on physical-goods mystery-box apps was found.
- The guidelines page showed no "last updated" date.
- Apple Pay's own merchant acceptable-use rules on gambling were not reviewed.

## 7. Google Play

### Takeaway
Google requires odds disclosure for randomized items "in advance of, and in close and timely proximity to" purchase, and disclosure of odds or the selection method for chance-based reward programmes. It **bans** unlicensed apps where users pay real money "to obtain a prize of real world monetary value". That wording is close enough to a paid physical mystery box that Play listing is a real risk. Physical goods are exempt from Play Billing.

### Cited Findings
- Apps offering randomized virtual items from a purchase, including loot boxes, "must clearly disclose the odds of receiving those items in advance of, and in close and timely proximity to, that purchase" (since 2019). — [Fenwick](https://www.fenwick.com/insights/publications/google-play-now-requires-disclosure-of-loot-box-odds); [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/games-on-the-google-play-store-now-required-to-disclose-loot-box-odds)
- For "variable, chance-based or randomised reward" programmes, developers must disclose odds where fixed odds are used, and the selection method otherwise. — [Google Play Developer Program Policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/17105854?hl=en-GB_nz) (via search summary)
- Real-money gambling is allowed only for licensed and approved apps that are free to download, don't use In-app Billing, and block minors and unauthorised locations. For all other apps, Google doesn't allow content where users "wager or participate using real money (including in-app items purchased with money) to obtain a prize of real world monetary value", including lotteries and games that take money and offer cash or other real-world-value prizes. — [Google Play Real-Money Gambling, Games, and Contests](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9877032?hl=en) (via search summary)
- Physical goods are outside the Play Billing requirement. — [Google Play Payments policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9858738?hl=en) (via search summary)
- No-purchase sweepstakes must disclose rules at first interaction (entry deadline, award date, number of winners). — [Google Play RMG policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9877032?hl=en) (via search summary)

### Inferences
- Before a Play launch, get a policy opinion, or keep paid draws on the PWA and ship a Play app that is vault, collection and shipping only. A paid draw for a figure with resale value could be read as "real money to obtain a prize of real world monetary value".
- Because odds change with stock, show odds on the purchase screen itself, not only on a separate page.

### Gaps
- The full current Play policy text could not be fetched (blocked). Wording should be checked against the live Policy Center.
- No Play enforcement against a physical mystery-box app was found.

## 8. Payment processors and card networks (Stripe, PayPal, Shopify, Visa/Mastercard)

### Takeaway
Mainstream processors **prohibit games of chance with a monetary or material prize**, or require prior approval. A mystery box is not named outright, but it sits close to those categories. Disclosing every possible item is the common condition for tolerance. If the product is classified as gambling, card rules require **MCC 7995** (high-risk) for *all* online transactions, which drastically changes approval rates, fees and processor options.

### Cited Findings
- **Stripe (prohibited):**
  - "games of chance including gambling, internet gambling, casino games, sweepstakes and contests, and fantasy sports leagues with a monetary or material prize";
  - games of skill with a monetary or material prize;
  - entry fees that promise a prize of value;
  - lotteries and bidding-fee auctions.
  - Charity sweepstakes and raffles are only "restricted".
  - Some listed businesses may be supported with explicit prior approval.
  - Sources: [Stripe Prohibited and Restricted Businesses](https://stripe.com/legal/restricted-businesses) (via search summary); [Stripe FAQ](https://support.stripe.com/questions/prohibited-and-restricted-businesses-list-faqs)
- A third-party integrator reports Stripe "tolerates" loot boxes if the product description clearly lists all possible items. *Unofficial source.* — [Tip4Serv docs](https://docs.tip4serv.com/gateways/stripe-prohibited-business)
- **PayPal:** activities with an entry fee and a prize (chance or skill), including sweepstakes, need prior approval. Gambling is defined to include games of chance "for money, property, or other things of value". These transactions are excluded from Purchase Protection. US gambling is otherwise banned except approved merchants who can block US and illegal jurisdictions. — [PayPal AUP](https://www.paypal.com/us/legalhub/paypal/acceptableuse-full); [PayPal Help](https://www.paypal.com/us/cshelp/article/what-gambling-activities-does-paypal-prohibit-help391)
- **Shopify:** no explicit mystery-box rule was found. Merchants report Shopify Payments being disabled for "mystery boxes and scoops", with itemised product descriptions suggested as a fix. Many mystery-box apps are listed in the Shopify App Store. Enforcement appears inconsistent. — [Shopify Community thread](https://community.shopify.com/c/shopify-discussions/shopify-legal-allows-certain-merchants-to-get-away-with-selling/td-p/1971334); [Shopify App Store: Mysbox](https://apps.shopify.com/mystery-box-builder)
- **Visa MCC 7995:** "Betting, including Lottery Tickets, Casino Gaming Chips, Off-Track Betting, Wagers at Race Tracks and games of chance to win prizes of monetary value." It requires high-risk registration. **Online gambling merchants must use 7995 for all transactions**, even when gambling is not their main business. Higher interchange and stricter controls follow. — [Visa Merchant Data Standards Manual (Apr 2026)](https://usa.visa.com/dam/VCOM/download/merchants/visa-merchant-data-standards-manual.pdf); [CommerceGate](https://www.commercegate.com/mcc-7995-compliance-guide-for-global-gambling-operators/); [eFlow](https://www.eflow.com/post/high-risk-mcc-codes-visa)
- Sweepstakes-casino laws (California AB 831, New York S5935) extend liability to payment processors. That gives processors a direct incentive to drop gambling-adjacent merchants. — [ZwillGen](https://www.zwillgen.com/gaming/californias-ab-831-bans-sweepstakes-casinos-expands-liability-vendors/); [Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/articles/new-york-enacts-law-prohibiting-sweepstakes-casinos/)

### Inferences
- Before launch, get **written approval or underwriting sign-off** from the processor for the exact model. Describe it as a retail sale of a randomly assigned figure from a fully disclosed list, with published odds and no cash-out.
- Keep a full public itemised list per tier: every piece, image, retail value and live odds.
- Adding buyback, cash-out, crypto, case battles or paid spins would likely (a) breach Stripe and PayPal terms and (b) push the business into MCC 7995. Most mainstream acquirers won't support 7995 for an unlicensed US operator.
- Expect higher chargeback exposure. PayPal excludes these sales from Purchase Protection, and unhappy-pull "buyer's remorse" disputes are likely. Clear pre-purchase disclosures and refund terms (Section 14) help win disputes.

### Gaps
- The full text of Stripe, Mastercard and Apple Pay rules could not be fetched.
- No source confirms whether any card network has issued guidance specifically on mystery boxes.
- Mastercard's equivalent rules (e.g. its high-risk registration programme) were not verified.

## 9. United Kingdom

### Takeaway
The Gambling Act 2005 treats loot boxes as gambling only where the prize is "money or money's worth". The Gambling Commission's test is **cash-out or tradability**. A physical figure arguably *is* money's worth, so a UK physical mystery box rests on product-sale framing and guaranteed contents. Buyback would be very hard to defend. The government chose **industry self-regulation in 2022** (Ukie principles, 2023). The **ASA** actively rules against hidden or misleading random mechanics in ads.

### Cited Findings
- Gambling Act s.6 defines gaming as "playing a game of chance for a prize". Under s.6(5), "prize" means money or money's worth. Loot-box items that stay in-game and can't be cashed out are unlikely to be licensable gambling. Convertible or tradable items attain "money's worth". — [House of Commons Library CBP-8498 (Aug 2024)](https://researchbriefings.files.parliament.uk/documents/CBP-8498/CBP-8498.pdf); [Gambling Commission](https://www.gamblingcommission.gov.uk/news/article/loot-boxes-within-video-games); [Springer, Psych/Legal analysis](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11469-020-00372-3)
- LexisNexis UK publishes a practitioner Q&A on whether "mystery box prize promotions with stated retail value" are permitted under the Gambling Act and advertising rules, which shows this is a live practitioner question. — [LexisNexis UK Q&A](https://www.lexisnexis.com/en-gb/legal/q-a/if-a-promoter-offers-a-mystery-box-of-prizes-with-an-x-amount-retail-value-is-this-permitted-under-the) (only the title was seen)
- **DCMS response (18 Jul 2022):** found an "association" between loot boxes and harms but no proven causation. It declined legislation and asked industry for protections. — [GOV.UK government response](https://www.gov.uk/government/calls-for-evidence/loot-boxes-in-video-games-call-for-evidence/outcome/government-response-to-the-call-for-evidence-on-loot-boxes-in-video-games); [Hansard WMS](https://www.theyworkforyou.com/wms/?id=2022-07-18.hcws211.h)
- **Ukie's 11 principles (2023):**
  - technological controls so under-18s cannot buy loot boxes without parental consent;
  - odds disclosure and design-led fair play;
  - lenient refunds where a minor spent without parental knowledge;
  - **no enforcement mechanism**.
  - Sources: [Ukie](https://ukie.org.uk/news/new-loot-box-principles-agreed-by-industry); [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=952157a6-a46b-4750-91a2-3dd2277130e4)
- Leon Xiao's longitudinal study found **non-compliance with and non-enforcement of** the UK principles on the Apple App Store. — [PMC: "Non-compliance with and non-enforcement of UK loot box industry self-regulation on the Apple App Store"](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12115820/)
- **ASA (Nov 2025):** upheld complaints against Kabam, Hutch Games and Nexters because app store listings did not clearly disclose loot boxes (material information). In Hutch's F1 Clash, a "Golden Spin" wheel **implied an equal chance of each prize** when the odds differed, which was misleading. — [ASA ruling on Hutch Games (F1 Clash)](https://www.asa.org.uk/rulings/hutch-games-ltd-a25-1282245-hutch-games-ltd.html); [Bird & Bird](https://mediawrites.twobirds.com/post/102j2eg/asa-bans-mobile-game-listings-for-failing-to-disclose-presence-of-loot-boxes); [Mishcon de Reya](https://www.mishcon.com/news/asa-rulings-uphold-two-claims-concerning-video-game-loot-box-disclosures)
- The ASA also told EA its loot-box disclaimer in ads must be clearer. — [NEXT.io](https://next.io/news/marketing/asa-ea-loot-box-disclaimer/)

### Inferences
- In the UK, a **spin wheel must not visually imply equal odds**. Segment sizes should match real probabilities, or show odds on the wheel.
- Every ad and app listing should say up front that "contents are randomly assigned".
- Get UK gambling counsel before launch. Physical figures are inherently money's worth, so "every buyer gets a disclosed item of value for the price" carries more weight in the UK than in loot-box analysis.

### Gaps
- No ASA ruling found specifically on a *physical* mystery-box site (e.g. HypeDrop or Lootie).
- No Gambling Commission enforcement action found against a physical mystery-box seller.
- Neither the 2017 Gambling Commission position paper nor the Ukie text was read in full.

## 10. European Union and member states (Belgium, Netherlands, Spain, CPC principles, DFA, KIDS Act)

### Takeaway
National treatment varies:
- **Belgium:** treats paid loot boxes as illegal gambling (since 2018).
- **Netherlands:** its highest administrative court overturned the EA FIFA fine (2022).
- **Spain:** a pending organic law would bar minors from paid random rewards that are exchangeable for money.

EU-wide, the CPC Network's **March 2025 principles** set consumer-law expectations for virtual currencies. The Commission's **EU KIDS Act proposal (17 Sep 2026)** would ban loot boxes for minors under 18 and require certified age verification. It is a proposal, not yet law.

### Cited Findings
- **Belgium:** the Gaming Commission found in **April 2018** that paid loot boxes (FIFA 18, Overwatch, CS:GO) were gambling and recommended prosecution. EA stopped selling FIFA Points in Belgium in 2019. — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/ea-amends-i-fifa-i-loot-boxes-in-belgium-after-regulators-increase-pressure); [NAG](https://www.nag.co.za/2018/04/26/belgium-gaming-commission-says-loot-boxes-in-three-games-violate-local-laws/)
- Xiao's study found Belgium's ban **ineffective** in practice because many games kept selling loot boxes. — [Collabra: Psychology, "Breaking Ban"](https://online.ucpress.edu/collabra/article/9/1/57641/195100/Breaking-Ban-Belgium-s-Ineffective-Gambling-Law)
- **Netherlands:** the Council of State ruled on **9 Mar 2022** that FIFA packs were not a game of chance under Dutch law, overturning the Kansspelautoriteit penalty against EA. Dutch parties later backed a proposed ban. — [Clifford Chance](https://www.cliffordchance.com/insights/resources/blogs/talking-tech/en/articles/2022/09/the-ultimate-loot-drop-the-netherlands-is-planning-to-ban-loot.html); [VGC](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/six-dutch-political-parties-unite-behind-proposed-loot-box-ban/)
- **Spain:** the Council of Ministers approved the Organic Law Project on protecting minors in digital environments (Mar 2025) and sent it to Congress.
  - Article 5 bars minors' access to loot boxes that require direct or indirect payment, have random outcomes, and give rewards exchangeable for money or other virtual objects.
  - Congress rejected Vox's amendment to the whole bill (178 against, 137 abstentions, 33 in favour).
  - Sources: [Infoplay (26 Mar 2025)](https://www.infoplay.info/es/2025-03-26/el-gobierno-aprueba-el-proyecto-de-ley-de-proteccion-de-menores-en-entornos-digitales-con-medidas-clave-sobre-videojuegos-y-plataformas-con-loot-boxes/31127/noticia/); [BOCG](https://www.congreso.es/public_oficiales/L15/CONG/BOCG/A/BOCG-15-A-52-1.PDF); [Audidat](https://www.audidat.com/blog/noticias/ley-proteccion-menores-entornos-digitales-redes-loot-boxes-delitos/)
- **CPC Network Key Principles on In-Game Virtual Currencies (21 Mar 2025):** non-binding, but they signal how EU authorities will enforce consumer law:
  - clear real-money pricing and pre-contract information;
  - no hiding costs or forcing currency purchases;
  - respect for the right of withdrawal;
  - attention to vulnerable consumers, especially children.
  - Industry (EGDF, Video Games Europe) objected.
  - Sources: [European Commission PDF](https://commission.europa.eu/document/download/8af13e88-6540-436c-b137-9853e7fe866a_en?filename=Key+principles+on+in-game+virtual+currencies.pdf&prefLang=hu); [Reed Smith Q&A](https://www.reedsmith.com/articles/qas-on-the-eu-consumer-protection-authorities-joint-guidance-paper/); [CMS](https://cms-lawnow.com/en/ealerts/2025/04/level-up-compliance-video-games-industry-in-the-spotlight-again-for-consumer-law-compliance)
- **Digital Fairness Act:** public consultation launched 17 Jul 2025. It covers "uncertainty-based rewards" paid with money or in-game currency, with options from parental consent to bans for minors. — [Freshfields](https://www.freshfields.com/en/our-thinking/blogs/technology-quotient/the-eus-proposed-digital-fairness-act-a-game-developers-guide-to-potential-imp-102ltio); [Osborne Clarke](https://www.osborneclarke.com/insights/digital-fairness-act-unpacked-specific-features-digital-products-particularly-those-common)
- **European Parliament (Oct 2025)** called for new EU measures to protect minors online, including on loot boxes. — [European Parliament press release](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/news/en/press-room/20251013IPR30892/new-eu-measures-needed-to-make-online-services-safer-for-minors)
- **EU KIDS Act proposal, COM(2026) 681**, published **17 Sep 2026**. It covers social networks, video-sharing, **online games**, AI chatbots and **app stores**, and defines a child as under 18. Reported provisions:
  - a **ban on loot boxes for minors**;
  - **certified age verification** instead of self-declaration;
  - no rewarding minors for regular log-ins or penalising them for stopping.
  - Sources: [Davis Wright Tremaine](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/privacy--security-law-blog/2026/09/eu-kids-act-online-child-safety-proposal); [Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/viewpoints/102o1h8/the-eu-kids-act-has-landed/); [Burges Salmon](https://www.burges-salmon.com/articles/102o1pk/the-eu-kids-act-not-just-a-social-media-ban-and-not-just-an-eu-problem/); [Servola](https://servola.de/journal/loot-boxes-for-minors-are-now-illegal-in-the-eus-own-draft-law/); [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/advocacy-group-stop-killing-games-sounds-alarm-on-eu-kids-act-this-is-such-a-horrible-law-proposal-2000738930)

### Inferences
- For any EU launch:
  - geo-block **Belgium** for paid draws;
  - set paid draws to **18+ with real age verification**, since the KIDS Act proposal and Spain's law point that way;
  - show coin values in euros;
  - build for the EU **14-day right of withdrawal**. Whether opening a box ends the withdrawal right for a physical good is a question for counsel.
- A free daily spin aimed at minors would conflict with the KIDS Act's ban on log-in rewards, if enacted.

### Gaps
- Not known: whether Spain's law has been finally passed and published in the BOE.
- Not known: the KIDS Act's exact definitions (whether a physical-goods shop with draws is an "online game").
- Not known: whether any EU authority has acted on physical mystery boxes.
- Not researched: the effect of the EU Consumer Rights Directive withdrawal right on opened blind boxes.

## 11. Australia

### Takeaway
Since **22 Sep 2024**, games classified with **paid chance-based items get at least M** (advisory, 15+) and games with **simulated gambling get R18+** (legally restricted). This is a computer-game classification scheme and probably doesn't directly cover an ecommerce PWA. The app stores apply it through regional ratings (Apple: 16+ for loot boxes in Australia).

### Cited Findings
- From 22 Sep 2024, games with loot boxes or chance-based in-game purchases get at least M, and simulated gambling gets R18+. The rules apply to games classified after that date. Chance mechanics without real-money purchase are exempt. M is advisory; R18+ is legally restricted. ### Inferences
- In Australia, gambling-law risk (state and territory lotteries and gaming) and Australian Consumer Law are likely bigger issues than classification. Neither was researched here.

- Australian state lottery and trade-promotion laws (e.g. NSW trade-promotion lottery rules) and the Interactive Gambling Act's application to physical mystery boxes were not researched.

## 12. China (SAMR blind box guidelines)

### Takeaway
China's **SAMR Guidelines for Business Conduct for Blind Boxes (Trial)**, issued June 2023, are the most product-specific rules anywhere for physical blind boxes:
- no sales to **under-8s**;
- guardian consent for ages 8–17;
- prominent disclosure of probabilities, distribution, limited-edition quantities and value range;
- operators are encouraged to set **guarantee ("保底")** mechanisms and spending, frequency and time caps;
- no hyping or feeding the secondary market.

### Cited Findings
- No sales to under-8s; sales to minors aged 8 and over need confirmed guardian consent. — [Beijing Business Today](https://www.bbtnews.com.cn/2023/0615/479345.shtml); [Xinhua](http://www.news.cn/fashion/20230719/317cbe6ddfa549f988e8cadc1956448d/c.html); [MOFCOM text](https://policy.mofcom.gov.cn/claw/clawContent.shtml?id=97054)
- Must prominently disclose, before purchase: product name, type and styles, draw rules, distribution, **number of limited items released**, **draw probabilities** and **value range**. — [MOFCOM interpretation](https://policy.mofcom.gov.cn/claw/policyInfo.shtml?id=5424); [Beijing legal-aid site](https://www.bj148.org/ztk/2024nzt/2022gjxfr/fwts/202403/t20240311_1663434.html)
- Operators are encouraged to set up a **guarantee mechanism** and to cap draw time, amount and number of purchases to promote rational consumption. — [Beijing Business Today](https://www.bbtnews.com.cn/2023/0615/479345.shtml)
- Pricing must be reasonable and based on cost and supply and demand. Price gouging is banned, and blind-box prices should not differ greatly from non-blind-box goods of the same quality. Operators should not hoard, hype, or deliver directly to the secondary market. — [Asiallians](https://asiallians.com/en/distribution-samr-issues-guidelines-for-business-conduct-for-blind-boxes-for-trial-implementation/); [STCN](https://www.stcn.com/article/detail/894058.html)
- Certain goods may not be sold in blind-box form (per SAMR commentary). — [STCN](https://www.stcn.com/article/detail/894429.html)
- **Shanghai's 2022 compliance guidelines** said a single blind box should generally not exceed **¥200**. The national guideline sets no specific price cap. A "¥1,000 cap" sometimes cited could not be confirmed. — [Shanghai SAMR](https://scjgj.sh.gov.cn/1008/20220802/2c984ad682591f7201825dd974c6257c.html); [Yahoo Finance / SCMP](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/shanghai-government-caps-price-blind-093000873.html); [Zhonglun](https://www.zhonglun.com/research/articles/9641.html)

### Inferences
- The SAMR model is a strong **best-practice template** for Blind Box even outside China:
  - show item value ranges and limited counts alongside odds;
  - add an optional pity or guarantee (e.g. a guaranteed higher-tier piece after N pulls);
  - add user spend and frequency caps;
  - stay out of resale.
- **Conflict to manage:** SAMR wants value-range disclosure, but Whatnot bans stating floor, ceiling or average value. Prefer disclosing **retail or MSRP per piece**, and avoid "EV", "profit" or "market value" framing.

### Gaps
- Not read in full: the SAMR guidelines' full article list, including any rules on online and live-stream sales and "prohibited categories".

## 13. Japan

### Takeaway
Japan's Consumer Affairs Agency banned **"kompu gacha"** (complete-a-set rewards) in 2012 under the Act against Unjustifiable Premiums and Misleading Representations. Ordinary gacha is legal and self-regulated by JOGA rules on odds disclosure and accuracy. Prizes offered **as premiums** through lottery-style promotions tied to purchases are capped by value.

### Cited Findings
- On 18 May 2012 the CAA declared kompu gacha prohibited "card matching" under the Premiums Act, effective 1 Jul 2012. — [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2012-05-18/japan-consumer-agency-says-complete-gacha-game-feature-illegal); [Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/performance-marketing/japan-officially-declares-lucractive-kompu-gacha-practice-illegal-in-social-games/); [Abe Legal](https://abe-legal.jp/en/columns/game-gacha-legal-regulation)
- JOGA guidelines require per-item probability disclosure and displayed rates that match actual rates. They set expected-cost guidance for rare items (reported as 100 draws or ¥50,000). — [GachaWiki](https://gachawiki.com/wiki/gacha-regulation) (wiki source); [Abe Legal](https://abe-legal.jp/en/columns/game-gacha-legal-regulation)
- **Premiums Act general-lottery ("一般懸賞") caps:**
  - transaction price under ¥5,000: maximum prize 20× the price;
  - ¥5,000 or more: maximum prize ¥100,000;
  - in both cases, total prizes no more than 2% of expected sales.
  - Sources: [Consumer Affairs Agency FAQ](https://www.caa.go.jp/policies/policy/representation/fair_labeling/faq/premium/lotteries); [J-Net21](https://j-net21.smrj.go.jp/qa/development/Q0006.html)

### Inferences
- In Japan, avoid any **"collect the full series, get a bonus figure"** mechanic (kompu gacha risk).
- Treat spin-wheel or coin prizes tied to purchases as premiums subject to the 20× or ¥100k and 2% caps.
- Japanese gambling law (Penal Code) on physical gacha was not researched. Get local counsel.

### Gaps
- Not found: whether the CAA treats an online *physical* blind-box item as the "product" or as a "premium".
- JOGA guideline texts were not read directly.

## 14. South Korea

### Takeaway
Since **22 Mar 2024**, the amended **Game Industry Promotion Act Art. 33** requires game companies to disclose loot-box probabilities in-game, in ads and on websites. Enforcement is active: hundreds of violations were found within months. Penalties have since been toughened (reported treble damages and revenue-based fines). Foreign game companies must appoint a local representative.

### Cited Findings
- Art. 33 GIPA was amended 21 Mar 2023, effective 22 Mar 2024. It requires probability disclosure on all platforms: in-game, in ads and on the official site. — [ScienceDirect (Xiao et al.)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0001691825008030); [ResearchGate version](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/395466350_Better_than_industry_self-regulation_Compliance_of_mobile_games_with_newly_adopted_and_actively_enforced_loot_box_probability_disclosure_law_in_South_Korea)
- 266 games were found violating the rules in the first months, and 185 titles fixed their disclosures. — [Game World Observer](https://gameworldobserver.com/2024/07/08/266-games-violated-loot-box-rules-south-korea)
- Reported later changes: **treble damages** (from ~2025) and proposed fines up to 3% of revenue (capped at ₩1bn) under a new Art. 38-2. *Secondary sources; check the statute.* — [Glitch Rant](https://www.glitchrant.com/south-korea-loot-box-law-revenue-fines/); [shattered.io](https://shattered.io/loot-box-odds-disclosure-laws-2026/)
- Foreign game companies must designate a local representative. — [Transatlantic Law](https://www.transatlanticlaw.com/content/korea-update-foreign-game-companies-to-be-mandated-to-designate-local-representative/)
- Korea's rules have pushed global platforms (e.g. Roblox) to disclose odds worldwide. — [Tech Times](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/319148/20260626/koreas-loot-box-rules-push-roblox-disclose-item-odds-worldwide.htm)
- Xiao's research found Korea's legally enforced disclosure produced better compliance than industry self-regulation. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0001691825008030)

### Inferences
- The GIPA applies to "games", so a physical-goods shop is probably outside it. Korea's standard is still the best benchmark for disclosure placement: odds on the purchase screen, in ads and on the website.

### Gaps
- Not determined: whether physical blind-box ecommerce falls under GIPA, the Korean E-Commerce Act, or the Fair Trade Commission's rules.

## 15. Advertising and influencer rules (sponsored openings, ads to minors)

### Takeaway
The verified rules on ads for random mechanics are: the UK ASA requires ads and listings to disclose random-item purchasing and not to imply equal odds; Korea requires odds in ads; the FTC's HoYoverse case targeted marketing to children. The FTC Endorsement Guides could not be fetched. As background, they require clear disclosure of material connections, including free product, in sponsored unboxings.

### Cited Findings
- ASA: app listings must clearly disclose the presence of loot boxes, and spin-wheel visuals must not imply equal chances. — [ASA, Hutch Games ruling](https://www.asa.org.uk/rulings/hutch-games-ltd-a25-1282245-hutch-games-ltd.html); [Bird & Bird](https://mediawrites.twobirds.com/post/102j2eg/asa-bans-mobile-game-listings-for-failing-to-disclose-presence-of-loot-boxes)
- Korea: probabilities must also appear in ads. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0001691825008030)
- FTC: loot boxes "unfairly marketed" to children were part of the HoYoverse complaint. — [FTC business blog](https://ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2025/01/level-tips-businesses-ftcs-settlement-genshin-impact-developer-hoyoverse)
- SAMR: discourages hype and secondary-market speculation. — [STCN](https://www.stcn.com/article/detail/894058.html)

### Inferences (background knowledge, not verified this session)
- FTC Endorsement Guides (16 CFR Part 255, updated 2023):
  - influencers who get free boxes, coins, affiliate fees or payment must clearly and conspicuously disclose it (e.g. "#ad", "sponsored", or "Blind Box gave me these boxes" spoken in video and shown on screen);
  - brands are responsible for monitoring influencer compliance;
  - the guides add specific caution about ads aimed at children.
- The 2024 FTC Rule on Consumer Reviews and Testimonials bans fake or bought reviews.
- **Rigged or curated "sponsored openings"** are a major deception risk. Influencer pulls must come from the same live odds as everyone else, or the video must disclose that they don't. Watch whether the dollar value of influencer accounts' pulls stays in line with the published odds.
- Don't target ads at under-18 audiences on TikTok, YouTube or Instagram.

### Gaps
- FTC Endorsement Guides and Consumer Reviews Rule texts were not read (ftc.gov and ecfr.gov blocked; search budget exhausted).
- No FTC or ASA action on paid influencer "unboxing" of mystery-box sites was found.

## 16. Practical compliance recommendations: odds format, draw audit, terms, age gating, geo-blocking, responsible play, refunds

### Takeaway
These are synthesised from the regimes above.

**Must-haves:**
- live, per-piece odds shown on the purchase screen;
- immutable draw logs;
- 18+ (or at minimum under-16 parental consent) for paid draws;
- no buyback or cash-out;
- geo-blocks where the risk is highest;
- clear refund and terms language;
- written approval from the payment processor and app stores before launch.

### Cited Findings (anchors for each recommendation)
- Odds disclosed per item before purchase: Apple 3.1.1 ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). Shown "in close and timely proximity to" purchase: Google ([Fenwick](https://www.fenwick.com/insights/publications/google-play-now-requires-disclosure-of-loot-box-odds)). Rates must match actual rates: JOGA ([Abe Legal](https://abe-legal.jp/en/columns/game-gacha-legal-regulation)). Odds also in ads: Korea ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0001691825008030)). Distribution, limited counts and value range too: China ([MOFCOM](https://policy.mofcom.gov.cn/claw/policyInfo.shtml?id=5424)).
- Misrepresenting odds is actionable deception (FTC v. HoYoverse). — [Pocket Gamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/hoyoverse-agrees-to-20-million-settlement-with-ftc-over-loot-boxes/)
- Age: under-16 parental consent (FTC HoYoverse order); no sales under 8 and guardian consent for 8–17 (SAMR); under-18 parental controls (Ukie); bans for minors proposed in Spain and the EU. — sources in Sections 5, 9, 10, 12
- Spending, frequency and time caps, plus a guarantee or pity mechanism: SAMR ([Beijing Business Today](https://www.bbtnews.com.cn/2023/0615/479345.shtml))
- Refunds and dark patterns: Epic order (express consent; findable refunds; no lockout over chargebacks) — [FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2022/12/245-million-ftc-settlement-alleges-fortnite-owner-epic-games-used-digital-dark-patterns-charge). Lenient refunds for unauthorised minor spending — [Ukie](https://ukie.org.uk/news/new-loot-box-principles-agreed-by-industry). EU right of withdrawal — [CPC principles](https://commission.europa.eu/document/download/8af13e88-6540-436c-b137-9853e7fe866a_en?filename=Key+principles+on+in-game+virtual+currencies.pdf&prefLang=hu)
- Full itemisation of possible contents: Whatnot policy ([Whatnot](https://help.whatnot.com/hc/en-us/articles/34107510260237-Surprise-Sets-Policy)); processor tolerance ([Tip4Serv](https://docs.tip4serv.com/gateways/stripe-prohibited-business), unofficial)

### Inferences (recommended specifications)

**1. Odds display (live shelf)**
- On every tier page *and* on the confirm-purchase sheet, show a table with: piece name, image, rarity tier, retail or MSRP value, units remaining, and current pull probability (e.g. "3 of 140 left: 2.14%").
- Show odds to two decimal places, and make sure displayed odds sum to 100%.
- Lock the odds snapshot to each order and show it on the receipt ("You drew at these odds: …").
- If stock changes between viewing and confirming, re-confirm or honour the displayed snapshot.

**2. Draw integrity and audit**
- Use a server-side cryptographically secure RNG.
- Keep an **append-only draw log** per order: timestamp, user ID, tier, stock snapshot hash, odds snapshot, RNG output and seed commitment, and the piece drawn.
- Consider commit-reveal ("provably fair") hashes, published so users can verify, plus periodic third-party statistical audits comparing realised against published rates.
- Keep the logs for at least the limitation period for consumer and gambling claims. Washington and California claims can reach back years, so ask counsel for the period.
- Reconcile physical inventory against the digital shelf. Selling a "chase" that isn't actually in stock is the classic deception risk.

**3. Terms of service**
- No purchase of coins; coins have no cash value, are non-transferable, cannot be redeemed for cash, and expire or are forfeited on account closure.
- No buyback.
- Random-assignment disclosure.
- Odds snapshot governs.
- Vault storage terms: title passes on draw, a storage period, a shipping-fee schedule, and abandoned-property handling. Unclaimed-property law may touch long-held vault items; check this.
- Refund policy, eligibility (18+), excluded jurisdictions, and dispute and arbitration terms. Note that mass arbitration is already being used against Whatnot.
- Official rules for the daily spin.

**4. Age gating**
- 18+ for paid draws. Use DOB at signup, plus card-holder name match, plus third-party age verification where the law requires it (EU KIDS Act if enacted; Texas App Store Act flows age signals from app stores).
- No under-13 accounts at all, to stay outside COPPA.

**5. Geo-blocking (paid draws), by IP, billing and shipping address**
- Block Belgium.
- Consider Washington and other high-litigation US states pending counsel review.
- Don't launch in China without a local entity and SAMR compliance.
- Add jurisdictions as counsel advises (e.g. Spain and the EU once the Spanish law and KIDS Act are final).

**6. Responsible-play features**
- User-set daily, weekly and monthly spend limits, defaulting on.
- Cool-off and self-exclusion.
- Spend history dashboard.
- Reality-check prompts after N draws.
- Optional pity or guarantee mechanic (SAMR-style).
- No "near-miss" animations or loss-disguised-as-win effects.
- Spin wheel segments proportional to real odds (ASA).

**7. Refunds**
- Clear policy for unopened, undrawn credit.
- Defective or damaged pieces get replacement.
- Generous refunds for unauthorised minor spending.
- No account lockout over chargebacks (Epic).
- EU withdrawal-right handling per counsel.

**8. Features to keep out**

| Status | Features |
|---|---|
| Never, without licences | Instant buyback or sell-back for cash or credit; credit or coin cash-out; case battles; paid spins or re-spins; crypto deposits; purchasable coins alongside "free" redeemable coins (sweeps model) |
| Caution | Resale integrations, "market value" badges, "complete the set" bonus rewards (Japan kompu gacha), log-in streak rewards for minors (EU KIDS proposal), influencer-only boosted odds |

**9. Where a lawyer is required**
- A US gaming opinion covering the guaranteed-value posture, tier pricing versus floor value, and Washington, New York and California exposure.
- Sweepstakes or official-rules review for the free spin.
- Processor underwriting submission.
- An App Store or Play pre-submission strategy.
- UK, EU, Spain, Japan and China local reviews before any launch there.
- COPPA and age-verification design.
- Vault storage and unclaimed-property structuring.

### Gaps
- No regulator-endorsed "safe harbour" design for physical online blind boxes exists in the US, UK or EU. These recommendations combine adjacent regimes.
- No source specifies record-retention periods for draw logs.
